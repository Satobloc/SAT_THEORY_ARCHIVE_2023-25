Third-Order Consequences
	1.	Apparent Equivalence-Principle Violations
Composition-dependent filament linking densities imply that bodies with different internal topological makeup will couple differently to both g_G(x) and to curvature (via unified action).  This yields tiny, but in principle observable, differential accelerations in precision free-fall tests—an emergent, topological violation of the weak equivalence principle derived from second-order gauge anisotropy and curvature coupling.
	2.	Frequency-Dependent Gravitational Wave Propagation
Gravitational waves traverse a medium defined by filament-induced g_{\mu\nu}(x).  Because strain fluctuations (S_{\mu\nu}, O7  ) introduce scale-dependent effective refractive indices, one expects dispersion and birefringence in the high-frequency tail of gravitational-wave signals—a clear SAT4D signature beyond pure GR predictions.
