So, the way I see it the primary argument for YSAT or something similar is likely to be either right or, at least isometric enough to whatever the real nature of the universe is that it is a plausible working model goes something like this:

Part I: Assumptions 
Start with the following assumptions:
1. All of the properties of a fundamental particle—or indeed, any object—can, in principle, be mapped as properties of a sufficiently detailed world-line/hyperstructure or group of the same; which is to say simply, that any observed property or behavior can be mapped as the properties of a time-extended hyperstructure. This can be done to an arbitrary degree of fidelity/isomorphism to any system in the real world. 
2. The converse is also true—that, in principle, the instantaneous state of any real world system can be represented arbitrarily accurately as the intersectional boundary of such a structure with a three-dimensional “time hypersurface”. 
Justifications for these assumptions: I think both of these “assumptions” are necessary consequences of the nature of mapping—that is, the fact that any system is, in principle, capable of mapping the structure of another system, so long as the conceptual richness of the mapping system is at least as great as the system being mapped, or that the mapping richness is allowed to increase to meet the mapped system’s degree of richness.
4. It would appear that the conceptual richness of four dimensional space is… Perhaps by definition… rich enough to map any 3+1D system; and generally any open higher dimensional space is inherently more rich than any lower dimensional space. As such, four dimensional space is sufficient for mapping all of three dimensional space; in other words: 4-space is the next-simplest territory sufficient to map the entire 3+1D universe, after 3+1D space. [Incidentally, the corollary must also be true: That mapping 3+1D systems to higher-than-4-dimensions introduces inherently unnecessary extra assumptions. It should, however, be stressed that the lack of necessity of higher dimensions does not in itself deprecate the potential utility of higher dimensional mappings, nor that the utility of using the minimal necessary mapping domain (ie, 4D for mapping 3+1D) is guaranteed to be greater than the utility of using higher dimensional mappings; and in fact, the opposite is probably true—that there is a trade-off between the number of unnecessary assumptions, and the degree of convenience a higher-than-required dimensional space offers for mapping with respect to the minimally-sufficient-dimensional mapping space for any given system.] 
Thus, any higher-than-3+1D model is sufficient to model any *purely* 3+1D (or lower) system, and any such model is equally or more predictive and requires fewer non-essential assumptions than any model of the same system into higher dimensions—whether or not is more convenient for other reasons.
Postulate:
If the preceding assumptions are true, then SAT or another four-dimensional model can, in principle, be developed that has equal or higher predictive value and fewer unnecessary (and therefore untestable) assumptions and complexities. 
Therefore, if it can be shown that SAT or a similar or dissimilar four-dimensional model can be developed such that it has equivalent “convenience” (by whatever definition of convenience one chooses) the that model will *necessarily* have both equal or greater predictive power and fewer unconstrained terms than any higher-dimensional model.
