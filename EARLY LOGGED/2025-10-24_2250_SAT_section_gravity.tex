\section{Emergent Gravitational Potential}
\label{sec:gravity_potential}

\subsection{SAT Embedding Result}

For a $Q=3$ Borromean bundle ensemble the SAT emergent metric
(Modules~\textbf{SAT.O6}--\textbf{SAT.O7}) leads to the static weak--field
potential
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{\SAT}(r)
  \;=\;
  -\frac{GM}{\sqrt{r^{2} + \varepsilon^{2}}},
\end{equation}
with $G$ the effective Newton constant, $M$ the bundle mass, and
$\varepsilon$ the minimal filament curvature radius
($\varepsilon \sim 10^{-20}\,\text{m}$ from Phase~VII lattice fits).
For $r \gg \varepsilon$ one recovers the Newtonian form
$\Phi_N(r) = -GM/r$; at $r\!\to\!0$ the potential is regular:
$\Phi_{\SAT}(0) = -GM/\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Numerical Comparison}

Using $\varepsilon = 10^{-20}\,\text{m}$ we numerically evaluated the
relative deviation
\(
\Delta(r) = \left|\Phi_{\SAT}-\Phi_N\right|/\left|\Phi_N\right|
\)
over $r \in [10^{-10},10^{+1}]\,\text{m}$:
\[
\Delta(r) < 10^{-4}\% \quad\text{for } r \gtrsim 10^{-14}\,\text{m}.
\]
Hence all current laboratory and astrophysical tests lie in the
indistinguishable regime.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Key consequences}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Correct classical limit:} SAT reproduces Newtonian gravity
        to within present experimental resolution.
  \item \emph{Regularised core:} curvature singularities are avoided,
        providing a UV--finite gravitational field.
  \item \emph{Falsifiability:} deviations could appear in ``fifth--force''
        experiments probing $r \lesssim 10^{-14}\,\text{m}$ or in
        black--hole horizon physics.
\end{itemize}
