Ultra-Porous “Cauliflower” Planetesimals:
a Natural Courier Class for Galactic Panspermia
Nathan J. Bellomy1 and ChatGPT-o32
1Independent Researcher

ABSTRACT
Streaming-instability collapse can form kilometre- to dwarf-planet bodies with bulk densities ρ ≲
0.5 g cm−3. We show analytically that objects up to ∼1.5×103km radius can retain porosity φ ≳0.7
for gigayears, giving surface gravity g ≲0.03 g⊕and escape speed vesc ≈260ms−1. (1) Thirtymetres
of such foam attenuate Galactic-cosmic-ray dose below 10kGy in 108yr, adequate for spore survival.
(2) Impact-melt pores provide ∼105yr warm, wet niches; with ∼1015 parallel sites, abiogenesis need
succeed only once. (3) Sub-kilometre fragments are lofted gently by ≲5kms−1 impacts without lethal
shock and then coast through interstellar space. (4) Aerogel-like porosity permits gas-drag capture into
young protoplanetary disks at 13kms−1. Conservative rates still yield ∼1010 shielded “spore loaves”
roaming the Milky Way and ∼0.003 deliveries to every newborn disk, making cauliflower bodies a
non-negligible panspermia channel. Objects in this class should show bulk densities ρ<0.3gcm−3, flat
optical colours, minimal thermal flaring, and ∼10−3mms−2 radiation-pressure accelerations detectable
by the Vera Rubin Observatory within the next decade.

5. DISCUSSION AND CAVEATS
Major uncertainties remain: (i) full SPH simulations
must verify porosity survival for M ≳1023kg; (ii) micro-
bial productivity of impact-melt pores is unmeasured;
(iii) capture efficiency depends on ISO velocities and
disk turbulence.
Even under pessimistic inputs the
mechanism supplies a few-per-thousand delivery rate;
under optimistic values it dominates Galactic pansper-
mia. Upcoming LSST, radar, and JWST observations
will decide.
