A Minimal Geometric Model for Topological Mass
Emergence

Abstract
We construct a compact field model in which inertial mass arises from the geometric
coupling between a tensioned filament network and a dynamical time–flow field. The
framework is formulated purely as a mathematical representation: no claim is made
regarding ontological reality. A self–consistent Lagrangian density is shown to yield
an emergent mass term that depends only on topological invariants of the underlying
structure. The resulting scaling law reproduces the observed mass hierarchy pattern up
to a multiplicative constant and yields a falsifiable prediction for curvature–dependent
inertial shifts at sub–cosmological scales.

Introduction
Classical and quantum field theories treat mass as an intrinsic property of matter fields or as a
parameter entering symmetry–breaking potentials. We explore here a minimal alternative: a
purely geometric representation in which effective mass emerges from the interaction between
a one–dimensional filament current Jµν and a unit time–flow vector uµ on a four–dimensional
background manifold. This construction, while not intended as an ontological claim, offers
a mathematically transparent way to visualize how inertia may appear as a measure of
topological misalignment.
