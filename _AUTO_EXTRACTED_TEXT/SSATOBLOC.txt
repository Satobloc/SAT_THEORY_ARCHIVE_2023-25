Scalar–Angular–Twist A Minimal Geometric Model for
Topological Mass Emergence
Nathan J. Bellomy
October 2025
Abstract
We construct a compact field model in which inertial mass arises from the geometric
coupling between a tensioned filament network and a dynamical time–flow field. The
framework is formulated purely as a mathematical representation: no claim is made
regarding ontological reality. A self–consistent Lagrangian density is shown to yield
an emergent mass term that depends only on topological invariants of the underlying
structure. The resulting scaling law reproduces the observed mass hierarchy pattern up
to a multiplicative constant and yields a falsifiable prediction for curvature–dependent
inertial shifts at sub–cosmological scales.

Introduction and Literature Review
Classical and quantum field theories treat mass as an intrinsic property of matter fields or as a
parameter entering symmetry–breaking potentials. We explore here a minimal alternative: a
purely geometric representation in which effective mass emerges from the interaction between
a one–dimensional filament current Jµν and a unit time–flow vector uµ on a four–dimensional
background manifold. This construction, while not intended as an ontological claim, offers
a mathematically transparent way to visualize how inertia may appear as a measure of
topological misalignment.

The Re-Emergence of Geometric Foundationalism
The Scalar–Angular–Twist Blockwave Model (SATO/Blockwave) is situated within
the emergent geometric spacetime paradigm, popularized by Eric Weinstein’s Geometric
Unity model, providing an alternate formulation to the discrete formulation developed in
Bruno Bourgoin’s Quantum Filament Theory (QFiT). Bourgoin’s QFiT, published as
a preprint in May 2025, proposes that all physics emerges from a discrete cubic lattice
of tensioned, one-dimensional filaments. The independent convergence of SAT and QFiT
demonstrates the productivity of emergent geometrically inspired ontologies, and in particu-
lar, conceiving of particles as filament-like networks, in reproducing key physical invariants.
The SATO/Blockwave model utilizes a similar filamentary ontology in a continuous four-
dimensional foliation governed by a time-flow vector uµ and a compact phase field θ. Mass
and inertia emerge from the topological coupling between filament flux Jµν and uµ, predicting
a scaling relation of meff ∝1/Q (Conversation History). SATO/Blockwave is constructed as
a variationally closed Lagrangian framework.
Independent Development and Chronology
The foundational Scalar–Angular–Twist (SAT) framework was conceived independently of
QFiT, rooted in conceptual reflections and efforts at 4-dimensional thinking originally in-
spired by String Theory, and first documented in foundational sketches in the author’s note-
books as early as 2003. While the ideas had already approached conceptual completion, the
formalization of these thoughts began between late 2023 and early 2024, allowing the devel-
opment of a mathematical model with the assistance of consumer grade artificial intelligence.
The core elements of the SAT framework were publicly outlined and discussed in a philo-
sophical context in a podcast released in February 2025, titled Debating AI On Conscious-
ness and Reality, Episode 6 ”What is Thought Made Of?”, preceding the May 2025 release
of the QFiT manuscript. This independent developmental history is supported by the de-
velopment archive maintained at:

Discussion and Outlook
The SATO–Blockwave1 formalism, interpreted here as a mathematical model, provides a
compact route from geometric assumptions to an explicit mass formula. Its strength lies in
simplicity: the emergent mass term follows directly from the alignment geometry without
the need for symmetry breaking. The construction is compatible with general covariance
and remains perturbatively stable under small curvature.
While this paper presents a minimalist interpretation of the SAT model framework, the
authors have elsewhere shown that the frameworks is sufficient to make many additional
predictions and retroductions, often with astonishing accuracy. Some of these are available
in the table that follows the current presentation.
Further work should evaluate the stability of the uµ sector beyond linear order and com-
pute numerical spectra for specific topological classes. Whether or not nature employs this
mechanism, the model demonstrates that topological coupling between a tensioned filament
field and a time–flow foliation is sufficient to generate an inertial term of the correct form.
