The freshest trends I’d point to are these:

Higher symmetries and SymTFTs. In high-energy theory and quantum field theory, there has been a major recent push toward “higher-form symmetries,” generalized global symmetries, symmetry topological field theories, and categorical symmetry. These are not just ordinary particle transformations; they concern transformations of extended objects—lines, surfaces, defects, boundaries. A 2025 JHEP paper, for example, treats “−1-form symmetries” within M-theory and SymTFTs, alongside mixed anomalies involving higher-form symmetries.
Higher gauge theory becoming more operational. The mathematics of higher gauge theory has existed for a while, but it is increasingly relevant because it naturally describes not only line holonomy, but surface holonomy and higher-dimensional transport. Schreiber’s 2025 higher-gauge-theory exposition explicitly frames this as higher-structure enhancements of ordinary gauge theory gaining attention.
Fractons and higher-rank gauge theories. In condensed matter, fracton phases and higher-rank gauge theories are a very active recent zone. These systems have constrained mobility and unusual conservation laws, so “what can move, how, and around what loops or surfaces” becomes central. A 2024 review describes gapless fracton liquids with higher-moment conservation laws that restrict individual charge and monopole mobility.
Quantum processors simulating topological order and anyons. This is one of the most concrete recent developments. Non-Abelian anyons used to be mostly theoretical or proposed in exotic materials; now groups are preparing and manipulating non-Abelian topological order in quantum processors. A 2024 Nature paper reports realization of non-Abelian topological order and control of anyons in a trapped-ion quantum processor. Quantinuum has also publicized creation and manipulation of non-Abelian anyons on present-day devices.
“Discarded” categorical sectors becoming useful. A very recent and conceptually striking trend is the revaluation of structures previously treated as null or irrelevant. A 2025 report on “neglectons” describes work in non-semisimple topological quantum field theory where components once discarded because of zero quantum dimension are reinterpreted as functional resources for topological quantum computation. That is uncannily close to the motif “≠ ∅”: what looked empty may not be operationally empty.
Gauge-equivariant and geometric deep learning. In machine learning, recent geometric deep learning increasingly imports bundle, gauge, equivariance, and transport language. A mathematical survey emphasizes group-equivariant and gauge-equivariant neural networks, developing the theory through principal bundles and associated vector bundles. Lattice gauge equivariant CNNs are another concrete example, using gauge-structured neural architectures for lattice gauge theory problems.
Emergent parallel transport in quantum state spaces. Recent work also treats parameter-dependent quantum states as undergoing something formally like parallel transport through Hilbert-space geometry. A 2024 Quantum paper derives emergent parallel transport and curvature for parameter-dependent quantum systems.

The freshest trends I’d point to are these:

Higher symmetries and SymTFTs. In high-energy theory and quantum field theory, there has been a major recent push toward “higher-form symmetries,” generalized global symmetries, symmetry topological field theories, and categorical symmetry. These are not just ordinary particle transformations; they concern transformations of extended objects—lines, surfaces, defects, boundaries. A 2025 JHEP paper, for example, treats “−1-form symmetries” within M-theory and SymTFTs, alongside mixed anomalies involving higher-form symmetries.
Higher gauge theory becoming more operational. The mathematics of higher gauge theory has existed for a while, but it is increasingly relevant because it naturally describes not only line holonomy, but surface holonomy and higher-dimensional transport. Schreiber’s 2025 higher-gauge-theory exposition explicitly frames this as higher-structure enhancements of ordinary gauge theory gaining attention.
Fractons and higher-rank gauge theories. In condensed matter, fracton phases and higher-rank gauge theories are a very active recent zone. These systems have constrained mobility and unusual conservation laws, so “what can move, how, and around what loops or surfaces” becomes central. A 2024 review describes gapless fracton liquids with higher-moment conservation laws that restrict individual charge and monopole mobility.
Quantum processors simulating topological order and anyons. This is one of the most concrete recent developments. Non-Abelian anyons used to be mostly theoretical or proposed in exotic materials; now groups are preparing and manipulating non-Abelian topological order in quantum processors. A 2024 Nature paper reports realization of non-Abelian topological order and control of anyons in a trapped-ion quantum processor. Quantinuum has also publicized creation and manipulation of non-Abelian anyons on present-day devices.
“Discarded” categorical sectors becoming useful. A very recent and conceptually striking trend is the revaluation of structures previously treated as null or irrelevant. A 2025 report on “neglectons” describes work in non-semisimple topological quantum field theory where components once discarded because of zero quantum dimension are reinterpreted as functional resources for topological quantum computation. That is uncannily close to the motif “≠ ∅”: what looked empty may not be operationally empty.
Gauge-equivariant and geometric deep learning. In machine learning, recent geometric deep learning increasingly imports bundle, gauge, equivariance, and transport language. A mathematical survey emphasizes group-equivariant and gauge-equivariant neural networks, developing the theory through principal bundles and associated vector bundles. Lattice gauge equivariant CNNs are another concrete example, using gauge-structured neural architectures for lattice gauge theory problems.
Emergent parallel transport in quantum state spaces. Recent work also treats parameter-dependent quantum states as undergoing something formally like parallel transport through Hilbert-space geometry. A 2024 Quantum paper derives emergent parallel transport and curvature for parameter-dependent quantum systems.
