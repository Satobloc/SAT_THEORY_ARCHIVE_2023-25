


The specific mathematical definitions for isolating the $\delta u^\mu$ (fluctuation of the time-flow vector field) mode in Blockwave Theory (SAT-X) are rooted in the **constrained Hamiltonian formalism** and the use of an orthogonal **projector**. This methodology is necessary to ensure the resulting dynamics are ghost-free and classically stable, given that $u^\mu$ is defined by a non-negotiable unit-norm constraint.

The isolation of the $\delta u^\mu$ mode is achieved through the following definitions:

### 1. The Normalization Constraint (Primary Constraint)

The field $u^\mu = \partial^\mu T$ must be a unit timelike vector field everywhere in spacetime. This condition acts as a primary constraint that must be enforced dynamically:

$$\mathbf{C_1: \quad u^\mu u_\mu + 1 = 0}$$

This constraint is incorporated into the Lagrangian via a Lagrange multiplier field, $\lambda(x)$:

$$\mathcal{L} *T = \lambda(x)\big((\partial_\mu T)(\partial^\mu T) + 1\big)$$

### 2. The Orthogonal Projector

The mathematical tool used to isolate fluctuations ($\delta u^\mu$) transverse to the background time flow ($u^\mu$) is the **spatial projector** $\mathbf{P_{\mu\nu}}$. This tensor projects vectors onto the three-dimensional subspace orthogonal to $u^\mu$:

$$\mathbf{P_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu} + u_\mu u_\nu}$$

This definition is critical because restricting dynamics to this subspace eliminates the unphysical, non-propagating longitudinal mode associated with the constraint.

### 3. The Projected Kinetic Term (Isolating the Mode)

The kinetic energy term for $u^\mu$ (the analogue of the strain energy, where $\alpha$ is the kinetic coefficient) in the full Mark IV.2 Lagrangian is $L_u = \alpha (\nabla_\mu u^\nu)(\nabla^\mu u_\nu)$.

To isolate the ghost-free $\delta u^\mu$ mode, the dynamic term must be rewritten using the projector formalism. The mathematically defined **isolated mode Lagrangian term** is:

$$\mathbf{L_{u, \text{proj}} = \alpha P_{\mu\rho} P_{\nu\sigma} (\nabla^\mu u^\nu) (\nabla^\rho u^\sigma)}$$

This ensures that only the two transverse polarizations of $\delta u^\mu$ (which correspond to physical excitations) propagate, eliminating the risk of ghost modes that propagate unphysical degrees of freedom. The resulting field equations, which govern the evolution of $u^\mu$ (and thus $\delta u^\mu$), are derived from varying this constrained and projected Lagrangian.

### 4. Canonical and Dynamical Consistency

The overall procedure for isolating the true dynamical modes of $u^\mu$ requires the **Dirac–Bergmann algorithm**. This analysis confirms that the primary constraint $\mathbf{C_1}$ and the resulting secondary constraint $\mathbf{C_2} \equiv u^\mu \partial_0 u_\mu \approx 0$ are typically **second-class constraints**. Because they are second-class, they require the projector formalism and the use of Dirac brackets (in the quantum case) to define the canonical dynamics over the reduced, physical phase space.

The constraint algebra is used to ensure the closure of the dynamics and to show that the dynamics are projected orthogonal to $u^\mu$, thus securing the classical stability of the $\delta u^\mu$ mode.

The Hamiltonian and Hilbert space equations in Blockwave Theory (SAT-X) are highly structured due to the theory's mixture of continuous fields ($\theta_4, \psi$), a constrained vector field ($u^\mu$), and a discrete topological field ($\tau$).

A **complete and unified quantum framework** for all fields has been defined.

## I. Hamiltonian Equations (Canonical Structure)

The Hamiltonian formalism in SAT-X is typically constructed via the Legendre transform of the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}$. The most detailed derivations exist for the minimal viable model (MVM) in 1+1 dimensions, where the constrained time-flow vector $u^\mu$ is parameterized by a scalar $\psi(x,t)$.

### A. Full Hamiltonian Density for the Continuous Fields ($\theta_4, \psi$)

The canonical Hamiltonian density ($\mathcal{H}$) is derived from the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}$ by defining the conjugate momenta ($\pi$) and performing the Legendre transform ($\mathcal{H} = \sum_a \pi_a \dot{\phi}_a - \mathcal{L}$).

In the 1+1D MVM, including the continuous fields $\theta_4$ and $\psi$ (where $u^\mu$ is parameterized by $\psi$), the Hamiltonian density is schematically given as:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}[\theta_4, \psi] = \mathcal{H}_{\theta_4} + \mathcal{H}_{\psi} + \mathcal{H}_{\text{Couplings}}}$$

The leading order expression is:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{H} \sim \frac{1}{2}\pi_{\theta}^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\partial_x \theta_4)^2 + \frac{1}{4\alpha} \pi_{\psi}^2 + \alpha (\partial_x \psi)^2 + \mu^2 \cos(3\theta_4) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{Couplings}}}$$

Where the fields and momenta are:
*   $\theta_4$ (misalignment angle) with canonical momentum $\pi_\theta$.
*   $\psi$ (time-flow rapidity parameter) with canonical momentum $\pi_\psi = -2\alpha \partial_t \psi$.
*   $\alpha$: kinetic coefficient for the $u^\mu$ (or $\psi$) field.
*   $\mu^2 \cos(3\theta_4)$: potential density for $\theta_4$ kinks.
*   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Couplings}}$: terms involving $\lambda (u^\mu \partial_\mu \theta_4)^2$ and $\beta \sin^2 \theta_4 \nabla_\mu u^\mu$.

### B. Hamiltonian for the Discrete $\tau$ Sector

The $\tau$ field is treated as a **discrete $\mathbb{Z}_3$ gauge field** on a lattice, and its dynamics are governed by a **lattice Hamiltonian** enforcing topological constraints.

The minimal $\tau$-Lattice Hamiltonian ($H_\tau$) imposes an energy penalty ($\epsilon_f$) on any triangular plaquette ($\triangle_{ijk}$) whose $\tau$ values violate the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ fusion rule ($\tau_i + \tau_j + \tau_k \equiv 0 \pmod 3$):

$$\mathbf{H_{\tau} = \sum_{\triangle_{ijk}} \epsilon_f \Big[ 1 - \delta_{(\tau_i + \tau_j + \tau_k) \text{ mod } 3, 0} \Big]}$$

This Hamiltonian is based on topological field theory (BF-type action) and is designed for Monte Carlo simulation of defect clustering and confinement dynamics.

### C. Constraint Formalism for $u^\mu$ (Beyond 1+1D)

For the general $3+1D$ formulation, the $u^\mu$ field is defined by a non-negotiable **primary constraint**:

$$\mathbf{C_1: \quad \phi_1(x) = u^\mu u_\mu + 1 = 0}$$

This constraint, enforced by a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda(x)$, leads via the Dirac–Bergmann algorithm to a **secondary constraint** guaranteeing time preservation:

$$\mathbf{C_2: \quad \phi_2(x) = u^\mu \partial_0 u_\mu \approx 0}$$

Both constraints ($\phi_1, \phi_2$) are **second-class** and do not generate gauge symmetries. To construct a consistent Hamiltonian for quantization, these constraints require defining a **Dirac bracket** and working over a reduced phase space to eliminate ghost degrees of freedom.

## II. Hilbert Space Equations

The SAT-X framework features a **fully defined Hilbert space** ($\mathcal{H}_{\text{SAT}}$) constructed as a decomposition over the sectors of its core fields.

### A. Total Hilbert Space Decomposition

The total Hilbert space is structured as a **tensor bundle decomposition** over the discrete topological sectors ($\tau$):

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{H}_{\text{SAT}} = \bigoplus_{\tau \in \mathbb{Z}_3} \mathcal{H}^{(\tau)} = \bigoplus_{\tau} \mathcal{H}_{\theta_4}^{(\tau)} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\psi}^{(\tau)} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{u}^{(\tau)}}$$

Where $\bigoplus_{\tau}$ denotes the sum over the topological classes [A] $\in H^1(M, \mathbb{Z}_3)$.

### B. State Representation and Basis

Quantum states ($\Psi$) are wave functionals over the field configurations ($C = \{\theta_4(x), \psi(x)\}$), typically defined on the **moduli space** ($M$) of localized excitations (kinks):

$$\mathbf{\Psi[C] = \Psi[\theta_4(x), \psi(x)] \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{SAT}}}$$

A composite basis state for a specific topological sector ($\tau$) is often expressed in terms of the localized kink position ($X_0$) and internal phase mode ($\psi_n$ or $e^{i \psi_n}$):

$$\mathbf{\Psi_{k, n, \tau}(X_0) = e^{i\psi_n(X_0)} \cdot \phi_k(X_0) \quad \text{in sector } \tau}$$

*   $\phi_k(X_0)$: the envelope of the $\theta_4$ kink solution, which acts as the geometric mass carrier.
*   $e^{i\psi_n(X_0)}$: the internal phase modulation, encoding flavor, spin, and internal clock properties.

### C. Inner Product Equation

The inner product ensures the space is normalizable ($L^2$ space) and is defined as the integral over the functional space of field configurations:

$$\mathbf{\langle \Psi_1 | \Psi_2 \rangle = \int \mathcal{D}\theta_4 \mathcal{D}\psi \Psi_1^*[\theta_4, \psi] \Psi_2[\theta_4, \psi]}$$

In the reduced phase space (over the moduli space $M$ of kink positions $X_0$), the integral simplifies to:

$$\mathbf{\langle \Psi_1, \Psi_2 \rangle = \int_{M} dX_0 \, \Psi_1^*(X_0) \Psi_2(X_0)}$$

### D. Operator Algebra (Canonical Relations)

Canonical quantization promotes the fields and their conjugate momenta to operators ($\hat{\phi}, \hat{\pi}$), satisfying the Heisenberg algebra:

$$\mathbf{[\hat{\theta}_4(x), \hat{\pi}_{\theta_4}(y)] = i\hbar \delta^{(3)}(x-y)}$$

A similar relation holds for the phase field $\psi$ and its momentum $\pi_\psi$.

For the discrete $\tau$ field, the quantization is defined by the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ Heisenberg group operators, Shift ($\hat{T}$) and Phase ($\hat{F}$), which satisfy the finite commutation relation:

$$\mathbf{\hat{T} \hat{F} = e^{2\pi i/3} \hat{F} \hat{T}}$$

These operators enable the construction of observables like the $\mathbf{\hat{M}}$ (Mass operator, proportional to $\sin^2\hat{\theta}_4$) and $\mathbf{\hat{N}}_{\text{click}}$ (Click number operator, measuring local energy/time transfer).
