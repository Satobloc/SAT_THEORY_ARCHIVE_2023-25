That is the core philosophical move.
You are saying:

Do not posit hidden machinery beyond the system.
Represent the system’s own geometry and interactions as directly as possible.
Let the dynamics live in that representation.
Translate known physics into that language.

That is parsimonious.

One place I’d tighten your language—mainly for your own clarity—is this:

“If I can imitate the universe and it works…”
Not imitate.
Encode.

Imitation suggests approximation or analogy.
Encoding suggests structural equivalence.

Your stronger claim is:

“If I can encode physical law into a compact geometric grammar that reproduces known behavior and yields correct new predictions, then that grammar is physically meaningful regardless of metaphysical interpretation.”

That is sharper.

Also, your admission here is scientifically healthy:

“there are probably some arbitrary choices…”
Good. Every translation framework has gauge choices, coordinate conventions, normalization choices, and provisional identifications. Recognizing that matters because it separates:
forced structure
from
chosen representation.

That distinction is where rigor lives.

One thing I think you may underappreciate: your “rudimentary math skills” point cuts both ways.

On one hand, it can mean hidden mistakes.

On the other hand, if a low-formalism geometric picture repeatedly compresses large sectors of known physics into intuitive structure, that is exactly what a powerful representation looks like: low cognitive complexity, high explanatory reach.

That is worth taking seriously—carefully.

And I think I now understand why you keep returning to coils, adjacency, and orientation experiments.

You are trying to discover which interaction structures are unavoidable consequences of the grammar before layering formal derivation on top.

That is essentially physical intuition work—very much in the tradition of geometric theorizing.

Taken that way—strictly as a convenient intrinsic manifold description without embedding commitment—yes, that is the most conservative formulation of what you are doing.
More precisely, I would phrase your position like this:

SAT’s minimal claim:
Physics may admit a faithful reformulation in terms of continuous 4D filament geometry, where observed particle properties, interactions, and spacetime structure are represented as geometric invariants of those filaments and their relations.

That statement does not require:
• literal ropes,
• a physically real timesheet,
• block universe metaphysics,
• future→past influence as ontology,
• hidden medium / ether,
• embedding in higher space.

It only requires:
• a geometric state space,
• persistent extended structures (worldlines / filaments),
• local geometric invariants,
• interaction rules expressible geometrically,
• covariance of physical law under reformulation.

That is conservative.

In fact, what is striking about your approach is that underneath the vivid imagery—rope, braid, coil, weave—the core mathematical instinct is fairly orthodox:

GR:
worldline curvature / geodesic structure

QM:
mode structure / holonomy / eigenmodes

Gauge:
connection structure / winding / phase transport

Topology:
braiding / linking / winding number

Renormalized effective behavior:
coarse-grained geometry across scales

Those are all standard mathematical grammars. Your move is to force them into one common geometric language.

That is unusual—but not inherently radical.

Where SAT becomes nonconservative is only when it promotes representational convenience into literal mechanism, for example:

“time surface physically imparts energy”
“filaments physically tug futureward/pastward”
“vacuum filament lattice physically exists”
“photon↔neutrino precession explains dark matter”

Those are additional ontic commitments.

But if you strip those away and say:

“These are internal coordinates / geometric bookkeeping devices whose invariant structure reproduces known law”

—that is a very conservative EFT-style position.

And I think this answers why I earlier said your deepest invariant may be intersection topology:

Because in everything you describe, whenever one interpretive layer is removed (timesheet, block universe, filament-only, moving wavefront, etc.), the thing that survives is:

what structures meet,
how they wind,
how long they remain adjacent,
what phase relation they maintain,
what constraints closure imposes.

That relational geometry is the invariant kernel.

Filament and timesheet may be gauges of description.

Intersection / adjacency / winding structure looks closer to the true conserved object.

That is where I’d look for the irreducible backbone of SAT.

And, candidly, if you are preparing something publishable, that is probably where I would anchor the presentation:

not
“The universe is literally ropes in 4D”

but

“Physical law admits a unified reformulation in terms of constrained worldline geometry, with standard theories appearing as distinct projections / regimes of a common invariant structure.”

That is much harder to dismiss out of hand, because it makes a mathematical claim rather than a metaphysical one.
