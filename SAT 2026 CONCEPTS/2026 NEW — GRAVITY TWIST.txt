What are planetary ephemerides

Planetary ephemerides are highly precise mathematical tables or models that describe the positions and motions of planets, moons, and other Solar System bodies over time.
In practice, they are time-indexed solutions to the equations of motion for bodies in the Solar System, refined using both physics and observation.

They are built from:

• Newtonian gravity plus relativistic corrections (primarily general relativity)
• continuous observational data (telescopic tracking, radar ranging, spacecraft telemetry)
• numerical integration of many-body gravitational interactions

Modern ephemerides are extremely accurate. For example, systems like JPL’s DE (Development Ephemeris) models can predict planetary positions with kilometer-level or better precision over long timescales.

What makes them important in this context is that they function as a consistency constraint on any proposed modification of gravity:

If a new physical effect existed that altered gravitational attraction in a history-dependent way, it would have to satisfy:

No detectable mismatch in planetary positions over decades to centuries of precise tracking
No unmodeled drift in orbital elements (semi-major axes, eccentricities, precession rates) beyond known perturbations
Compatibility with spacecraft navigation data (which is often sensitive to very small force errors)
Because these models are tuned simultaneously to many bodies, they effectively “lock in” a shared gravitational parameter set for long-bound objects.
So when people say “ephemerides rule out X,” what they mean is:

any new effect must be small enough, or structured enough, that it does not spoil the extremely tight agreement between predicted and observed planetary motion.

In your earlier framing, this is why a history-dependent gravity effect becomes tightly constrained: Earth, Mars, Jupiter, etc. all have billions of years of shared gravitational history with the Sun, so they all must fall on nearly identical effective parameters in whatever model is used.
