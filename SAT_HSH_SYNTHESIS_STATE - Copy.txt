# SAT/H(s)H Working Synthesis State

This is the current theory-architecture map after the second full corpus ingestion. It is a status document, not a claim that the physical model is complete. Source provenance, historical branches, contradictions, and detailed audits remain in `INGESTION_LEDGER.md`.

## 1. Governing discipline

1. Standard empirical physics is the benchmark and input constraint, not an opponent.
2. Ontology, modeled object, mathematical representation, numerical implementation, and observable readout remain distinct.
3. A geometrical translation inherits no novel empirical credit merely by reproducing an input equation.
4. New physical claims must identify the dependency that produces them, their free parameters, comparison model, uncertainty, and a holdout test.
5. User statements, sourced mathematics, generated proposals, executable identities, fitted representations, and speculative extensions carry separate provenance and maturity labels.
6. Failed equations remain in a failure registry so their constants or repair terms cannot silently re-enter later work.

## 2. Empirical entry layer

The disciplined entry point is the experimentally successful relativistic description:

- events and observer-dependent coordinates;
- Lorentzian bilinear form and invariant interval;
- proper time and timelike/null/spacelike classification;
- light cones and causal order;
- observer simultaneity hypersurfaces;
- particle trajectories as worldlines, with the worldline recognized as a controlled centerline approximation when finite extent matters.

This layer must be stated in conventional mathematics before any proposed Euclidean, radial, higher-dimensional, or emergent representation is introduced. A Euclidean angle, ordinary norm, or positive tangent covariance does not provide Lorentzian causal structure.

## 3. Modeled-object hierarchy

The current hierarchy is:

1. **Empirical object:** event/trajectory/worldline or standard field configuration.
2. **Finite-core candidate:** an embedded history with centerline or history-surface, transverse profile, boundary, material frame, and interaction range.
3. **Full modeled configuration:** one or more finite-core histories plus medium/environment fields and boundary conditions.
4. **Representation:** an H(s)H/framed-curve or related parametrization of the configuration.
5. **Resolving operation:** slice, projection, intersection, detector response, or coarse-graining map.
6. **Readout:** the conventional observable reconstructed from that operation.

Centerline curvature, internal coiling, material twist, braid organization, finite-core contact, topology, and interaction dynamics are separately typed. A plotted braid records relative histories; it is not automatically the interaction that caused them. Open histories do not become topological links without a specified closure or periodic identification.

## 4. Microscopic dynamical candidate

The current minimal trial family is a finite-core, shell-time action of the schematic form

`S = integral d tau ds [ (mu/2)|partial_tau X|^2 - (T/2)|partial_s X|^2 - (kappa/2)|partial_s^2 X|^2 - U(X,frame,medium) ]`

with additional pair/contact terms for distinct histories and separately modeled medium fields when required.

The indispensable distinctions are:

- shell/observer evolution parameter `tau` versus material/arclength coordinate `s`;
- inertia `mu`, first-gradient tension/network response `T`, and bending stiffness `kappa`;
- exact constraints versus finite penalties;
- local/self energy, direct interfilament energy, and medium-mediated response;
- centerline degrees of freedom versus cross-sectional/frame degrees of freedom.

The fields, dimensions, symmetries, constitutive functions, and boundary data are not yet closed. The action is therefore a candidate family, not a final model.

## 5. Stability, propagation, and causal-readout bridge

For an equilibrium configuration, the quadratic fluctuation operator should take a form such as

`D(omega,k) = -omega^2 M + k^2 K + k^4 B + M0^2`.

Here `M` is the kinetic matrix, `K` the first-gradient/network response, `B` the bending response, and `M0^2` the Hessian of local/interacting potentials. This operator is the proper place to study:

- stable and unstable modes;
- propagation speeds and dispersion;
- rank loss and bifurcation;
- thresholds and state transitions;
- whether an effective characteristic cone exists.

A Lorentzian readout has not yet been derived. It must arise from the characteristic/principal-symbol structure together with a declared clock and observation map. Setting `K/M=c^2` by hand is a calibration or premise, not an internal result.

## 6. H(s)H and analytical representations

H(s)H is currently a representation family for finite-core histories, not the underlying object and not a declaration that every history is helical. It must expose, where applicable:

- centerline/history-surface geometry;
- frame and transverse structure;
- scale, pitch, rotation, curvature, and torsion data;
- closure, phase, and holonomy data;
- resolution and readout metadata.

The Universal Indicatrix is provisionally a scale/rotation trajectory generator. Generic four-dimensional rotations have two invariant planes and two angles; a physical generator must be selected by the action or input problem.

Whirligig/Donut is provisionally an analytical comparison/transport construction. Before it can function as a derivative or equation solver it needs:

1. a typed encoding from input equations/solutions to geometric objects;
2. an invariant correspondence rule between the objects;
3. an explicit geometric operator;
4. a decode/readout map;
5. exact benchmarks and failure cases;
6. controlled numerical examples compared with ordinary differentiation or solution methods.

## 7. Topology and interaction grammar

Topology is introduced only after the physical object and permitted transformations are stated.

Useful current tools include:

- framed-tube relation `Lk=Tw+Wr`, without identifying these quantities with centerline torsion or genus;
- torus and Klein fundamental groups and orientability data;
- Euler characteristic for boundaries, handles, and crosscaps;
- Morse critical points for creation, splitting/merging, and removal of resolved components;
- finite-core contact plus phase, energy/barrier, and branch-pairing conditions for reconnection;
- rank loss as an event candidate, not by itself a physical reconnection law.

Direct finite-core contact/reconnection (“Interbraid” in the current vocabulary) and long-range medium response (“Electrogravity” candidate family) remain separate interaction channels.

## 8. Resolution and emergence programme

The full-resolution model, parametrization, resolving map, and effective observable must be distinguished. The proposed programme is:

1. define the microscopic configuration and action;
2. solve or approximate it at the finest justified resolution;
3. declare a coarse-graining operator and scale;
4. derive effective fields, stresses, metrics, or counts;
5. test neighboring regimes where an identity changes;
6. quantify information loss and non-uniqueness;
7. compare with the conventional effective theory.

Strand count, constituent count, recursive order, topological invariant, and resolution-dependent effective count are separate. The old scalar `Q` is retired as a universal carrier of all of them.

## 9. Downstream research dossiers

### Particle and gauge dossier

Target: determine whether stable modes and interaction symmetries of the finite-core action can reproduce observed particle states, spin/statistics, charges, masses, mixing, and scattering. Root-of-unity phases, `Q` assignments, fixed mass ladders, and named gauge groups are not inputs unless independently justified. Pauli exclusion requires exchange antisymmetry or an equivalent spinorial state-counting result; event-horizon analogy is insufficient.

### Gravity and cosmology dossier

Target: derive a controlled microscopic-to-effective map from configuration/medium stress to a metric or equivalent observable gravitational response. Vortex/medium response, differential radial development, rotation-plus-expansion, reciprocal particle/black-hole scales, Klein/torus cosmology, and black-hole recycling remain parallel candidates. Dimensional comparisons use `H L` versus `c`, never `H+c`.

### Compact-object transition dossier

Target: test whether finite-core/medium dynamics admits invariant state boundaries, hysteresis, reconnection, or phase transitions in black holes and neutron stars. Forward-pull, streamers/dropout, ER-like interiors, finite time-front slabs, temporons, and macro-to-micro transitions remain speculative until an action, conservation ledger, and observation map exist.

### Quantum foundations dossier

Target: specify the probability and measurement model. If superdeterminism is retained, define the setting-dependent hidden-state distribution and derive Bell correlations plus operational no-signalling. Shared past light cones alone are not the model.

### Materials and optical dossier

Target: only after a constitutive finite-core/medium model exists, map it to standard response functions and test whether it compresses or predicts material/optical observables. Standard formulae relabeled with SAT variables are benchmarks, not new consequences.

## 10. Pulsar pin

Pulsar timing propagation and neutron-star glitches are two separate downstream programmes.

### Glitches

The viable H(s)H/SAT contribution is a deterministic state-transition model class:

- internal state/reservoir vector `z` with loading law `dot z=F(z, torque, temperature, ...)`;
- independently derived stability margin `m(z)`;
- event condition such as `m(z)=0`;
- rapid redistribution/reset map `z_plus=R(z_minus)`;
- relaxation and adaptation kernels;
- angular-momentum conservation across resolved stellar reservoirs;
- observation operator to spin frequency, spin-down, pulse phase, and radiative data.

The comparison ladder is standard glitch physics -> mechanism-agnostic threshold/reset model -> finite-core H(s)H mechanism. Event timing alone does not identify the trigger because loading, moving thresholds, and recovery kernels can be degenerate.

The historical surface-velocity model remains only a falsification baseline. `v_crit=Bc`, `0.246 rad`, lattice disengagement, `1-x^-3` as moment-of-inertia fraction, derivative-as-event-rate, and universal luminosity blackout are not active equations.

### Propagation residuals

A separate timing-propagation dossier may test path-dependent medium/metric perturbations. It requires a covariant propagation operator, a derived sky/time correlation kernel, comparison against clock/ephemeris/interstellar/noise/GW models, and honest exclusion limits. The historical `100 ns`, `100 pc` clump template is not a prediction.

## 12. Immediate mathematical bottlenecks

1. Specify one finite-core configuration space and all independent fields.
2. Write one dimensionally complete candidate action with boundary conditions.
3. Derive its Euler-Lagrange equations and conserved quantities.
4. Choose a tractable equilibrium and compute the quadratic operator.
5. Determine stability and characteristic structure without inserting the desired cone.
6. Define the resolving/readout map to one standard benchmark.
7. Validate the H(s)H encoding and analytical-tool round trip on that benchmark.
8. Only then promote one downstream application, with pulsar glitches a strong event-dynamics test case but not the first foundation calculation.
