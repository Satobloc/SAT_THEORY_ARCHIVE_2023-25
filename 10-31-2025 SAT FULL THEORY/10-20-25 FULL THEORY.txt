where \(W(\Phi)\) is the potential per unit mass (units of \(\Phi\)), \(\kappa\) is the curvature coupling, and \(\lambda\) is dimensionless. The independent fields for the variational principle are taken to be
\(\rho(\mathbf x,t),\,\mathbf v(\mathbf x,t),\,\Phi(\mathbf x,t)\).
Mass conservation (the continuity equation) is imposed as a constraint using a Lagrange multiplier \(\chi(\mathbf x,t)\). The constrained action is
\begin{equation}\label{S}
S[\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi,\chi]
= \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\!\!\int_{\Omega}
\Big\{ \mathcal L(\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi)
+ \chi\big(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\big)
\Big\}\,d^3x\,dt .
\end{equation}
Below we compute the stationary conditions \(\delta S=0\) under arbitrary compactly-supported variations
\(\delta\rho,\;\delta\mathbf v,\;\delta\Phi,\;\delta\chi\).
We integrate by parts and drop boundary terms (assuming either vanishing variations on the boundary or appropriate boundary conditions).

\textbf{1. Variation with respect to the Lagrange multiplier \(\chi\).}
Varying \(S\) with respect to \(\chi\) enforces the continuity constraint:
\[
\delta_{\chi}S = \int \delta\chi\;(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v))\,d^3x\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{ \; \partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v) = 0 \; . \;}

\textbf{2. Variation with respect to the velocity field \(\mathbf v\).}
Collect \(\mathbf v\)-dependent pieces of the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset \tfrac{1}{2}\rho \mathbf v^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)
+ \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v).
\]
Take the variation \(\mathbf v \mapsto \mathbf v + \delta\mathbf v\). Compute term-by-term.
(i) Kinetic term:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(\tfrac12\rho \mathbf v^2\bigr)
= \rho\,\mathbf v\cdot\delta\mathbf v \qquad (\rho \text{ treated as independent}).
\]
(ii) Interaction term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr)
= \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\delta\mathbf v)
= -\lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi)\cdot\delta\mathbf v
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)} .
\]
(We integrated by parts once and discarded the boundary term.)
(iii) Constraint term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\bigr)
= \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\,\delta\mathbf v)
= -\rho\,\nabla\chi\cdot\delta\mathbf v
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)}.
\]
Summing (i)--(iii) and requiring stationarity for arbitrary \(\delta\mathbf v\) gives the Euler--Lagrange condition:
\[
\boxed{ \;\rho\,\mathbf v - \lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi) - \rho\,\nabla\chi \;=\; \mathbf 0 \;. \;}
\]
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{vel_eq}
\boxed{ \;\mathbf v = \nabla\chi + \frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi)\;. \;}
\end{equation}
This equation expresses the velocity field in terms of the multiplier \(\chi\) (a Bernoulli-type potential) and the interaction-gradient term. It is the canonical momentum relation coming from the variational principle.

Collect \(\rho\)-dependent terms from the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset \tfrac12\rho\mathbf v^2 - \rho W(\Phi) + \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v
+ \chi(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)).

(ii) From potential term:

\[

S[q] = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(q(t),\dot q(t),t)\,dt.
\]
\subsection*{Symmetry hypothesis}
Suppose there is a one-parameter family of transformations
\[
q^i(t) \mapsto q^i(t) + \varepsilon\,\Delta q^i(q,t) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),
\qquad t \mapsto t + \varepsilon\,\Delta t(q,t) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),
\]
under which the Lagrangian changes at most by a total time derivative:
\[
L(q+\varepsilon\Delta q,\dot q + \varepsilon \Delta\dot q, t+\varepsilon\Delta t)
= L(q,\dot q,t) + \varepsilon\,\frac{d}{dt}F(q,t) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),
\]
for some function \(F(q,t)\). (When \(F=0\) the Lagrangian is strictly invariant.)
\subsection*{Variation of the action}
Compute the first-order change in \(S\) under the infinitesimal transformation. Let \(\delta q^i = \varepsilon\Delta q^i\) and \(\delta t = \varepsilon\Delta t\). The induced variation of the action (keeping fixed integration endpoints in the original parameter \(t\)) is
\[
\delta S = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \Big( \frac{\partial L}{\partial q^i}\delta q^i
+ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta\dot q^i + \frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\delta t \Big)\,dt.
\]
Integrate the $\delta\dot q^i$ term by parts:
\[
\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta\dot q^i\,dt
= \left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta q^i\right|_{t_1}^{t_2}
- \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{d}{dt}\!\Big(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Big)\delta q^i\,dt.
\]
Collecting terms and using the Euler–Lagrange equations
\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{dt}\!\Big(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Big) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q^i} = 0\),
the bulk integral vanishes for variations that follow the equations of motion. The remaining boundary contribution plus explicit total-derivative change of the Lagrangian (the \(F\)-term) yields
\[
\delta S = \left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta q^i\right|_{t_1}^{t_2}
+ \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(F - L\,\delta t\bigr)\,dt.
\]
Thus, for an on-shell trajectory (satisfying EOM) and when the action is invariant (i.e. $\delta S=0$ for arbitrary fixed-endpoint symmetry variations), we get
\[
\left.\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Delta q^i - F + L\,\Delta t\right)\right|_{t_1}^{t_2} = 0.
\]
Because the times \(t_1,t_2\) are arbitrary, the quantity in parentheses is constant in time. Define the Noether charge (conserved quantity)
\[
Q \;=\; \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Delta q^i - F + L\,\Delta t.
\]
Then \(dQ/dt = 0\) along solutions of the Euler--Lagrange equations. This is Noether's theorem in mechanics.
\section*{B. Field-theoretic Noether theorem (classical fields)}
Let \(\phi^a(x)\) be a finite collection of fields on spacetime (coordinates \(x^\mu\), \(\mu=0,\dots,d-1\)), with Lagrangian density \(\mathcal{L}(\phi,\partial_\mu\phi,x)\). The action is
\[
S[\phi] = \int_\Omega \mathcal{L}(\phi,\partial\phi,x)\,d^dx.
\]
\subsection*{Infinitesimal transformation and Lagrangian variation}
Consider an infinitesimal continuous transformation parameterized by \(\varepsilon\):
\[
x^\mu \mapsto x'^\mu = x^\mu + \varepsilon\,\Xi^\mu(x) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
\phi^a(x) \mapsto \phi'^a(x') = \phi^a(x) + \varepsilon\,\Delta\phi^a(x) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Equivalently we can describe the field variation at the same spacetime point,
\[
\delta\phi^a(x) \equiv \phi'^a(x) - \phi^a(x) = \varepsilon\,\hat\Delta\phi^a(x)
\]
where \(\hat\Delta\phi^a(x) = \Delta\phi^a(x) - \Xi^\mu(x)\partial_\mu\phi^a(x)\) (this subtracts the effect of the coordinate change).
Assume the Lagrangian density changes by at most a total divergence:
\[
\delta\mathcal{L} \equiv \mathcal{L}'(x) - \mathcal{L}(x)
= \varepsilon\,\partial_\mu F^\mu(x)
\]
for some local \(F^\mu(x)\). This is the standard invariance condition: action is invariant (up to boundary terms).
\subsection*{Variation of the action and current construction}
Compute the first-order change in the action under the transformation. The variation of the action (for compact domain or decaying fields so boundary terms vanish after including divergence) is
\[
\delta S
= \int_\Omega \biggl( \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^a}\delta\phi^a
+ \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\delta(\partial_\mu\phi^a)
+ \partial_\mu(\mathcal L\,\Xi^\mu) \biggr)\,d^dx,
\]
where the \(\partial_\mu(\mathcal L\,\Xi^\mu)\) term accounts for the change in the integration measure due to the coordinate shift \(x\mapsto x+\varepsilon\Xi\). Expand \(\delta(\partial_\mu\phi^a) = \partial_\mu(\delta\phi^a)\) and integrate the derivative term by parts:
\[
\int_\Omega \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\partial_\mu(\delta\phi^a)\,d^dx
= -\int_\Omega \partial_\mu\!\Big(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\Big)\delta\phi^a\,d^dx
+ \text{(boundary)}.
\]
Collect the bulk terms:
\[
\delta S = \int_\Omega \Bigg\{ \Big(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^a} - \partial_\mu\!\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\Big)\delta\phi^a
+ \partial_\mu\Big[ \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\delta\phi^a + \mathcal L\,\Xi^\mu \Big] \Bigg\} d^dx.
\]
If the fields satisfy the Euler--Lagrange equations,
\[
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^a} - \partial_\mu\!\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)} = 0,
\]
then only the total divergence term survives on-shell. Using the invariance assumption \(\delta\mathcal L = \partial_\mu F^\mu\) we can equate (on-shell)
\[
\partial_\mu\Big[ \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\delta\phi^a + \mathcal L\,\Xi^\mu \Big] = \partial_\mu F^\mu.
\]
Bring the right-hand side over to the left and define the Noether current
\[
\boxed{ \;J^\mu(x) \;=\; \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\hat\Delta\phi^a
\;+\; \mathcal L\,\Xi^\mu \;-\; F^\mu \; }
\]
(notice we used $\hat\Delta\phi^a=\delta\phi^a$ the field variation at fixed $x$). Then the invariance condition implies the local conservation law (on-shell):
\[
\boxed{ \;\partial_\mu J^\mu(x) = 0\;. \;}
\]
This is Noether's theorem: every continuous symmetry (leaving the action invariant up to a divergence) yields a conserved current \(J^\mu\). The associated conserved charge is
\[
Q = \int_{\Sigma} J^0(x)\,d^{d-1}x
\]
where \(\Sigma\) is a spacelike Cauchy surface; time evolution plus \(\partial_\mu J^\mu=0\) implies \(dQ/dt=0\) (under suitable boundary conditions).
\subsection*{Comments and important special cases}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Global internal symmetry:} if \(\Xi^\mu=0\) and \(\delta\phi^a=\varepsilon\,\Delta\phi^a\) with \(\delta\mathcal L=0\) (strict invariance), then the Noether current reduces to
\[
J^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\Delta\phi^a .
\]
Example: phase rotations of a complex scalar field give electric charge current.
\item \textbf{Spacetime translations:} take \(\Xi^\mu = \varepsilon^\mu\) (constant), \(\delta\phi^a = -\varepsilon^\nu\partial_\nu\phi^a\). With \(F^\mu=0\) one obtains the canonical energy--momentum tensor
\[
T^\mu_{\ \nu} = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^a)}\partial_\nu\phi^a - \delta^\mu_{\ \nu}\mathcal L,
\]
and conservation \(\partial_\mu T^\mu_{\ \nu}=0\) (Noether currents associated to translations). \(T^{00}\) is the energy density; \(T^{0i}\) are momentum densities.
\item \textbf{Gauge (local) symmetries:} if the parameter \(\varepsilon\) is a function of spacetime, the naive current vanishes on-shell (Noether second theorem) and instead yields identities among the Euler--Lagrange equations (gauge identities). For local gauge symmetries, conserved charges require careful handling (Gauss-law constraints).
\end{itemize}
\section*{Concise proof summary}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Start with an infinitesimal symmetry transformation of fields and coordinates that changes the Lagrangian by a total divergence.
\item Compute the variation of the action and integrate-by-parts the derivative terms.
\item Use the Euler--Lagrange equations to kill bulk terms; the remaining piece is a spacetime divergence.
\item Define the Noether current as the combination appearing inside the divergence; invariance implies its divergence vanishes on-shell.
\item Integrate $J^0$ over spatial slice to obtain a conserved charge.
\end{enumerate}

For gauge theories and the subtleties of local symmetries see treatments of the Noether second theorem and constrained Hamiltonian systems (Dirac-Bergmann formalism).

\section*{Mass–Hierarchy Derivation in the SAT/Blockwave Framework}
\subsection*{1. Bare filament mass scale \(m_0\)}
In the SAT/Blockwave model the fundamental filament tension is denoted by \(T\) (units: energy per length, or \(ML\,T^{-2}\)), and the characteristic filament scale by \(\ell_f\) (length). A corresponding bare mass scale is
\[
m_0 \;\sim\; \frac{T\,\ell_f}{c^2}\,,
\]
since \(T\,\ell_f\) has units of energy (\(ML^2T^{-2}\)), dividing by \(c^2\) yields a mass (\(M\)). Thus
\[
\boxed{ \;[m_0] = M,\quad m_0= \frac{T\,\ell_f}{c^2}\,.}
\]
\subsection*{2. Topological invariant \(Q\)}
Define the topological invariant
\[
Q = \bigl(1 + |L_{\rm link}|\bigr)\;\times\;\bigl(1 + |W_{\rm writhe}|\bigr)\;\times\;(1 + n_{\rm wind})\;\times\;(1 + \deg(P_{\rm Jones}))\,,
\]
a dimensionless integer (or near-integer) classifying filament bundle topology. For simplicity one may approximate
\[
Q \approx L_{\rm link} + W_{\rm writhe} + n_{\rm wind}\,,
\]
for minimal excitations.
\subsection*{3. Topological mass suppression law}
The dominant (“topological”) contribution to the effective mass is postulated as
\[
m_{\rm topo} = \frac{m_0}{Q}\,.
\]
This expresses the idea that the bare filament mass is suppressed by the inverse of topological complexity.
\subsection*{4. Induced inertial mass \(m_{\rm ind}\)}
Coupling of the filament bundle to the timesheet (foliation) field \(u^\mu(x)\) yields an induced inertia. In leading static limit one finds (via integrating out quadratic fluctuations \(\delta u^\mu\)):
\[
m_{\rm ind} \approx C\,\frac{g_1^2}{M^2}\;E_{\rm overlap}\,,
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(g_1\) is a dimensionless drag/inertia coupling constant,
\item \(M^2\) is the mass-gap squared of the \(u^\mu\) fluctuations: \(\omega^2 = k^2 + M^2\),
\item \(E_{\rm overlap}\) is the energy scale at which the filament bundle overlaps / sources the \(u^\mu\) field,
\item \(C\) is a dimensionless geometric constant.
\end{itemize}
Under units: \([E_{\rm overlap}] = \text{energy}=ML^2T^{-2}\); dividing by \(M^2\) (units \(T^{-2}\)) yields \(ML^2T^{-2}\times T^2 = ML^2\); multiply by dimensionless constants gives a mass scale \(ML^2 / L^0 = M\). So units are consistent.
\subsection*{5. Total effective mass}
\[
\boxed{ m_{\rm eff} = m_{\rm topo} + m_{\rm ind} = \frac{m_0}{Q} \;+\; C\;\frac{g_1^2}{M^2}\;E_{\rm overlap}\,.}
\]
\subsection*{6. Topological dominance condition}
We demand:
\[
\frac{m_{\rm ind}}{m_{\rm topo}} \;\equiv\; R \;\ll\; 1\,.
\]
That is,
\[
\frac{C\,g_1^2\,E_{\rm overlap}/M^2}{m_0/Q}
= \frac{C\,g_1^2\,E_{\rm overlap}\,Q}{M^2\,m_0}
\;\ll\; 1.
\]
Given numerical values for \(g_1,\,E_{\rm overlap},\,M^2,\,m_0,\,Q\) one can bound the regime of validity.
\subsection*{7. Quadratic-spectrum calibration formula}
Particle excitations labelled by a topological Q-charge \(Q\) and a vibrational quantum number \(N\) obey:
\[
m^2 = k\,(N + \beta\,Q^2 - a)\,,
\]
where \(k,\beta,a\) are calibration constants (with units \(M^2\)). In SAT variables we set
\[
k \sim \frac{T\,\ell_f}{c^2}\cdot\frac{1}{Q_0}\;,
\]
for a reference topological charge \(Q_0\), and solve:
\[
m = \sqrt{k\,(N + \beta Q^2 - a)}\,.
\]
Matching ground-state masses fixes \(k,\beta,a\); excited states \(N=1,2,\dots\) yield the mass spectrum hierarchy.
\subsection*{8. Scalar phase-field mass and vacuum energy}
From the \(\mathcal L_{\theta_4}\) block we define the canonical scalar field \(\phi \equiv \sqrt{f_\theta}\,\theta_4\). Its mass squared is:
\[
m_\phi^2 = \frac{\Lambda_\theta^4}{f_\theta}\,,
\]
and the vacuum energy density is
\[
\rho_\Lambda = m_\phi^2\,f_\theta = \Lambda_\theta^4\,.
\]
Thus \(\rho_\Lambda\) acts as a cosmological constant term in the emergent Einstein equations and is tied by the same filament tension scale via \(\Lambda_\theta^4 \sim T\,\ell_f\).
\subsection*{9. Summary of the mass-hierarchy result}
\[
\boxed{ m_{\rm eff}(Q,N) \;\approx\; \frac{T\,\ell_f}{c^2\,Q}
\;+\; C\,\frac{g_1^2}{M^2}\;E_{\rm overlap}
\;,\quad
m^2 \approx k\,(N + \beta Q^2 - a)\,. }
\]
To apply:
\begin{itemize}
\item Choose {\(T, \ell_f, g_1, M^2, E_{\rm overlap}\)} from the filament microphysics.
\item Tabulate minimal stable topological charges \(Q=2,3,4,\dots\) for particle families.
\item Use \(N=0\) for ground state, \(N=1,2,\dots\) for excited modes.
\item Verify \(R = m_{\rm ind}/m_{\rm topo} \ll 1\).
\item Predict masses \(m_{\rm eff}\), compare with observational masses; adjust \(k,\beta,a\) accordingly.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Dimensional consistency checklist}
\[
[m_0] = M,\quad [Q]=1,\quad [m_{\rm topo}]=M,
\]
\[
[m_{\rm ind}] \sim C\,\frac{g_1^2}{M^2}\,E_{\rm overlap}
\;\rightarrow\; \frac{1}{T^{-2}}\,\times (ML^2T^{-2}) = M L^2 \cdot T^0 ?
\]
In practice the overlap energy is localized in a volume \(\ell_f^3\), so \(E_{\rm overlap}\sim T\,\ell_f\), giving \(m_{\rm ind}\sim C\,g_1^2\,T\,\ell_f/(M^2c^2)\approx M\) with appropriate conversion factors. Hence units reduce to mass. The quadratic formula \(m^2 = k(\dots)\) is manifestly \(M^2\) on the left if \(k\) has units \(M^2\).
\section*{Operational interpretation}
\begin{itemize}
\item Topological suppression means the greater the linking/writhe/winding complexity \(Q\), the smaller the resulting inertial mass.
\item Induced mass is small correction from coupling to the timesheet field; for stable particles the topological part dominates.
\item The quadratic spectrum formula allows modeling of excited states (e.g., meson resonances, baryon excitations) by increasing \(N\) while keeping \(Q\) fixed.
\item The scalar phase field mass \(m_\phi\) ties the vacuum energy (cosmological constant) to the same filament/tension physics underlying particle mass generation.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Caveats and next steps}
\begin{itemize}
\item Precise values of \(T,\,\ell_f,\,M^2,\,E_{\rm overlap}\) require a full filament microphysical model and numerical coarse-graining.
\item Quantization of \(Q\) for observed particle families must be calibrated: e.g., why leptons correspond to \(Q=2\), baryons to \(Q=3\)?
\item The coupling to the timesheet field (giving \(m_{\rm ind}\)) requires the spectrum of \(\delta u^\mu\) excitations in the background and knowledge of overlap integrals.
\item The vacuum energy relation demands that \(\Lambda_\theta\) scale is consistent with cosmological observations (i.e., extremely small vacuum energy demands strong suppression).
\end{itemize}
