# SAT Archive Reading Plan

Restored from migration hold: .[⚙️_AI_FILES]/_MIGRATION_HOLD/.AI_READING_PLAN/READING_PLAN.md
Original created date: 2026-05-31

Purpose: provide a structured, read-only route through the SAT Theory archive, aimed at understanding the theory in its most effective current form while preserving room for deliberate digressions into odd, generative, or historically important material.

## Working Principle

Do not read the archive alphabetically. The archive is sedimentary: current theory statements, older formulations, derivational attempts, audit protocols, public-facing essays, conversations, and stray conceptual artifacts are interleaved. The right method is to build a map first, then read along the map.

The reading should proceed in layers:

1. Orientation layer — identify the current public/core shape.
2. Canonical theory spine — read the strongest theory statements.
3. Developmental layer — trace how the concepts evolved.
4. Formalization/audit layer — inspect the mathematical and critic-hardened form.
5. Domain dossiers — group material by physics area.
6. Nooks and crannies — deliberately wander into side files and strange branches.
7. PDF pass — after the text map exists, place PDFs into conceptual buckets before reading them deeply.

## Phase 7 — PDF Pass

PDFs should not be treated as unreadable black boxes, but they should be handled after the text map exists.

Approach:

1. Index PDF filenames.
2. Assign each PDF to a conceptual bucket.
3. Extract text when needed.
4. Compare PDF claims against newer text files before treating them as current.
