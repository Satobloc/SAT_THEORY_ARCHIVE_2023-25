Icis a rather brisk spring afternoon when Jack first encounters the giant skull. He sits perched atop his coughing John Deere Tractor and squints across the barren fields. Behind him, rows of blades turn the earth, making it new again for the crop of soybeans to be planted in a few weeks. He thinks quietly to himself, carrying along a slow careful dialogue in his head. The life of a bachelor farmer is a solitary one, but one to which our hero, should we deign to call him that, has long since adjusted. Jack's afternoon reverie is interrupted by a hideous scraping from behind him, much like the sound of a fork being dragged across a chalkboard. He mashes the brake and the clutch and yanks the trundling machine out of gear. He climbs down from the tractor and steps past the plough blades, peering at the careful furrows of earth between his dirty boots.
