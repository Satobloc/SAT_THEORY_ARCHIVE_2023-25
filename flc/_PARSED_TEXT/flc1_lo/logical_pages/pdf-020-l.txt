Joe

“Respect the pig,” father began as always, hands clasped together, head slightly bowed. “We, unlike other creeds, know from whence our bounty flows. Respect the pig, our sacred heart, our life, our joy. Amen.”

The pig was kept in the basement where they washed it and fed it according to its pleasure. It was average pig I suppose. Some families had more robust, more capable and more productive pigs. Ours, well, it was adequate.

The pig ate money. And not just any money. It had no taste for money that was not seasoned with sweat and sacrifice. It snorted at windfalls and cash gifts. It was a fairly picky pig. But in return for it’s feeding the pig provided. Yes, the pig provided.

The first time I actually saw the pig was when grandmother died. Mother held my hand and we went to Petition the pig of some assistance.

Mom had a few notes she managed to wrestle from Lady Bee downtown in exchange for some housecleaning. She offered them up to the pig and I watched amazed as it devoured them. As it did so its skin glowed a bright pink. Then she tubbed its belly and preyed that it provide a coffin for her
