He stood at the exit peering out across the employee parking lot. The lion was nowhere in sight

Others were already dashing for their vehicles. Some darted between cars, clutching their briefcases hoping to spot the lion before it spotted them. It could be crouching behind that blue BMW or that grey Chrysler.

Amold didn’t know. He did know the doors of the office were auto locked at precisely five p.m. and unless the cat took someone else soon he'd be running for it with all the other schmucks. He thought a bit of overtime might give him an edge. Maybe the cat would snag another while he was still exiting the building. However, the delay may have worked against him. The crowd was thinning now and the quickest employees had already made it to their vehicles and were speeding off for home.

He glanced at his watch. In moments when the doors locked he'd have to run for it anyway. So he tucked his briefcase up under his arm and made a dash for the first row of parking spaces.

There was a time when he'd been the first out of the building. Almost daring the cat to make a go for him. Now though, after years with the company, he felt a bit old and knew he was slower then he"d ever been. .

Yesterday a senior VP had been snagged. He hadn’t even been able to disarm his auto alarm before the lion was on him. He’d crumbled under the weight of the lion and had disappeared beneath its huge frame and full mane. Lucky for him he’d known the magic word and upon speaking it the lion released him with only the slightest bit of damage.

Amold knew the magic word also. But that was small comfort. It might work, it might not.

He gained the first row easily. One man skittered passed, eyes wide in terror, apparently he forgot where he parked and was now running haphazardly around searching for his car.
