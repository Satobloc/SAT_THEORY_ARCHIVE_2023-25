He stood at the exit pecring out across the employee parking lot. The lion was nowhere in sight.

Others were already dashing for their vehicles Some darted between cars, clutching their briefcases hoping to spot the lion before it spotted them, It could be crouching behind that blue BMW or that grey Chrysler.

Arnold didn’t know. He did know the doors of the office were auto locked at precisely five p.m. and unless the cat took someone else soon he'd be running for it with all the other schmucks. He thought a bit of overtime might give him an edge. Maybe the cat would snag another while he was still exiting the building. However, the delay may have worked against him. The crowd was thinning now and the quickest employees had already made it to their vehicles and were speeding off for home.

He glanced at his watch. In moments when the doors locked he’d have to run for it anyway. So he tucked his briefcase up under his arm and made a dash for the first row of parking spaces.

There was a time when he'd been the first out of the building. Almost daring the cat to make a go for him. Now though, after years with the company, he felt a bit old and knew he was slower then he'd ever been. .

Yesterday a senior VP had been snagged. He hadn’t even been able to disarm his auto alarm before the lion was on him. He’d crumbled under the weight of the lion and had disappeared beneath its huge frame and full mane. Lucky for him hed known the magic word and upon speaking it the lion released him with only the slightest bit of damage.

Arnold knew the magic word also. But that was small comfort. It might work, it might not.

He gained the first row easily. One man skittered passed, eyes wide in terror, apparently he forgot where he parked and was now running haphazardly around searching for his car.

What was that? A blur of movement to the right.

Doors slammed, engines started, wheels squealed and transmissions whined as employees reached the safety of there vehicles and sped off.

Now Amold was getting scared. The crowd was thinning and the lion had yet to show itself. It must be stalking someone particular. Beyond the third row of cars was the grassy median between the front and back parking lot. This open area was the most dangerous and the great cat had caught more than one employee here.

He stopped near a ford explorer and was just about to make a run for it when the lion leapt from behind a yellow VW, It covered the distance of the grassy median on seconds. Tail high, paws slapping the ground its mane fluttered with the speed of its attack.

Arnold held up his briefcase trying to deflect the attacking feline but it did nothing to slow the force of three hundred pounds of fluff and muscle.

They rolled to the ground, the cats maw firmly gripping his shoulder.

Amold froze. The cats sharp teeth pressed into his shoulder.

What was it? He thought frantically. The cat gently mawed his neck as if savoring the taste before biting straight into the meat.

What was it? What was it? What was the word.

The lion bore over him holding him down with one massive paw.

“Tilt,” Amold said remembering what the VP had told him. The magic word was tilt. “Tilt...tilt...tilt...” he repeated.
