Benjamin

Tangled in the wrinkled sheets my mother tucked in so long ago, I forfeit sleep to the gurgling lawn machinery. Outside, my neighbors push their mowers and weed whackers. They will sift slow release fertilizer granules into their spreaders and maybe apply a lime treatment to brighten their lawns into glimmering emeralds. They will homogenize their 4,000 square-feet into the collective neighborhood. They will persist.

It’s Sunday morning, the weekend, what they work for. It’s the motivation that pushes them through the stacks of paperwork at the office - the thought that induces reverie during Power Point presentations.

When you’re young you get by with the party, working to slap the check card down on bar tabs. The monotony disappears in frothy mugs of beer. The older you get, you seek quicter, more reticent retreats.

I will never grow old. I resign to stare through the glaze of my eyes at the spackled webs across the ceiling. Oh, the cacophony. | moan at the throb of my brain receding from the skull. Sunlight hovers like a specter against my beige curtains, feeling for cracks to spill into.

I put on the jeans and tee shirt balled on the floor and sit on the edge of my bed scraping the crusted sleep from my eyes. I part the curtains and squint into the light. Outside the race continues, sane Sam across the street has a mound of fecal scented in his driveway, He wears a golf hat and a striped shirt
