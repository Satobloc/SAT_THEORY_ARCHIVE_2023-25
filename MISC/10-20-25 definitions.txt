These sources concern the Scalar-Angular-Torsion (SAT) theory, a unified geometric framework proposing that all fundamental physics, including matter, forces, and spacetime, emerges from a four-dimensional network of particle worldlines called filaments interacting with a propagating time surface. Documents outline the theory's mathematical rigor, including deriving its dynamics from a single unified Lagrangian and initiating a phase of numerical derivation to match constants like the Newtonian constant of gravitation ($G$) and the W-to-Z boson mass ratio. Conceptually, the SAT theory defines fermions as structural filaments and bosons as dynamic torsional patterns (including the Temporon for time flow), while predicting the existence of two novel stable particle states defined by topological charge that serve as dark matter candidates. The source material emphasizes that SAT claims to be a parameter-free model that must prove its dimensionless ratios are fixed irrespective of the single physical anchor used to set the absolute scale, a requirement relevant for its intended submission to Physical Review D.
