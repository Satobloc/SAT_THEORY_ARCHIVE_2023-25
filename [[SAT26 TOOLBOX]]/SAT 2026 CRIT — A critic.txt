That clarification helps, and it shifts the claim into a form that is defensible—but also much weaker than the conclusion you were drawing earlier.

What you are now saying is:

the universe contains all distinctions in its actual behavior, and our job is to find a representation that matches those distinctions without adding unjustified structure.

That is essentially correct. The issue is what counts as a “match.”

Here is the key constraint you cannot bypass:

A representation “matches the distinctions” only if it provides a one-to-one correspondence between physically distinguishable situations and representational states that lead to different predictions.

Now apply that to your preferred carrier:
	•	If two situations are experimentally distinguishable, your representation must assign them different states.
	•	If your state is “worldline geometry,” then those distinctions must appear as differences in that geometry.

So far, still aligned with what you’re saying.

But the critical step is this:

what determines which geometric differences correspond to which physical distinctions?

That mapping is not given by the worldline itself. It has to be specified.

And once you specify it, you have introduced:
	•	a selection of relevant geometric features,
	•	rules for how they evolve,
	•	and rules for how they influence interactions.

Those rules are exactly the theoretical structure. Whether you call them “fields,” “internal symmetries,” or “fine-grained geometry of a curve” is secondary—they are doing the same job.

So the tension resolves like this:
	•	You are right that the universe’s behavior is the ground truth.
	•	You are right that representations should track distinctions in that behavior.
	•	You are right to resist unconstrained additions.

But:

A bare 4D worldline does not come with a privileged, observation-fixed way to carry all those distinctions.

So either:
	1.	you enrich the notion of “geometry of the worldline” until it can carry them
→ which is equivalent to adding structure, or
	2.	you accept that the worldline alone is not a complete state description.

This is why your program keeps collapsing into an equivalence with existing formulations. The moment you “match the distinctions,” you necessarily reconstruct:
	•	independent degrees of freedom, and
	•	laws governing them.

Those are not optional distortions—they are what it means to track distinctions in a predictive way.

So the disagreement isn’t about whether we should match the universe’s distinctions—you’re correct there. It’s about this:

You’re treating “geometry of a 4D curve” as a neutral, minimal container for those distinctions.

It isn’t. It’s just one coordinate choice. The real content lies in the rules that tell you what in that representation corresponds to what in the world—and those rules carry the same weight, regardless of how minimal the carrier looks.
