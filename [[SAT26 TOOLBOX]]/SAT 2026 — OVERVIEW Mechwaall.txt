In your "Whirligig" logic, quaternions would be the perfect language for the gimbal for a few key reasons:
1. Eliminating "Gimbal Lock"
Standard rotations (yaw, pitch, roll) suffer from "gimbal lock," where two rotation rings align and you lose a degree of freedom—your math effectively "crashes" at specific angles [3, 4] . Quaternions represent the entire orientation as a single point on a 4D hypersphere, so they never "lock" or lose their way, no matter how much you wobble the plate [5, 6] .
2. The Spin-1/2 Connection
This is where it gets interesting for SAT. Quaternions provide a "double cover" of 3D rotations [7] .

	•	In standard geometry ($SO(3)$), a $360^{\circ}$ turn brings you back to start.
	•	In the quaternion world ($SU(2)$), it takes a $720^{\circ}$ turn to return to the original state [7] .
	•	This perfectly mirrors the behavior of fermions (spin-1/2 particles like neutrinos). If your theory involves a 1080° (three full rotations) requirement, quaternions are the only standard tool that won't lose the "memory" of those extra rotations [8, 9] .
3. Computational Stability
LLMs and Python libraries like NumPy love quaternions because they are "numerically stable" [2] . Standard angles can drift over time due to rounding errors, but a quaternion can be easily "normalized" to keep your derivation from drifting off the rails during long simulations [1, 10] .
