Right. There are two different attribution problems, and they should not be collapsed.

One is technical: exports, screenshots, nested replies, copied text, missing timestamps, and formatting can make it unclear which person literally typed a line.

The other is intrinsic: the conversation itself was recursive. You echoed each other, finished each other’s formulations, altered phrases, repeated jokes, and absorbed one another’s language. A phrase may have had a first typist without having a clean single author.

So we should not force every item into “Nathan said” or “Amanda coined.” Better categories are:

* directly attributable;
* probably attributable;
* jointly developed;
* repeated or adopted from the other person;
* attribution unclear.

And even “directly attributable” should mean only that the surviving log shows who typed that instance—not necessarily who originated the phrase.

That is especially important for the Vernacular. Its nature may be less a lexicon invented by two separate speakers than a language produced in the space between them.
