1. The grammar of the Minkowski diagram, stated precisely
An event is a point X^μ in a 4-manifold with metric ds² = c²dt² − dx² − dy² − dz². A worldline is a timelike curve X^μ(τ), parameterized by proper time τ (defined so dτ² = ds²/c²— proper time is arc length in this geometry, nothing else). From this curve you can extract, at every point, a Frenet-Serret-like frame in 4D: the tangent u^μ = dX^μ/dτ (four-velocity, normalized u·u = c²), and its derivative a^μ = du^μ/dτ (four-acceleration, orthogonal to u by the normalization constraint). The "angle" a worldline makes with the time axis is rapidity, φ = artanh(v/c); boosts are hyperbolic rotations that preserve ds² — the direct analogue of ordinary rotation, just with cosh/sinh instead of cos/sin because the invariant form is indefinite. Curvature of the worldline (rate of change of u^μ) is proper acceleration; a geodesic (zero curvature) is inertial motion; in curved spacetime, geodesic curvature relative to the background is gravity, by construction, not by analogy.

That's the whole native grammar. Everything else has to be built from position, tangent, and their derivatives.

2. What follows natively from that grammar — no elaboration needed
Any quantity that's a function of X^μ(τ), u^μ(τ), a^μ(τ) alone: elapsed proper time (aging, time dilation), momentum via p^μ = mu^μ, force via dp^μ/dτ, rest mass via the invariant length of the momentum vector (p·p = m²c⁴), causal order (light cone structure), and gravitational free-fall (geodesic deviation). All of Newtonian and special-relativistic point-particle mechanics falls straight out. This is the part of your program I already agreed is essentially just correctly-applied Minkowski geometry.

3. Three hard boundaries — not hand-waves, each backed by a specific reason the bare grammar structurally cannot cross it
(a) Internal quantum numbers. At a given τ, the curve supplies exactly: position (4 numbers), tangent direction (3 independent, given the normalization), and however many derivatives of curvature/torsion you choose to track. That's a finite stack of continuous geometric data. Electric charge, color, and (as I'll get to) spin-as-a-label are discrete data — eigenvalues of an internal symmetry group acting on some space other than position-and-tangent. A bare curve has no discrete internal state at all unless you build one into it. This isn't optional elaboration, it's a real gap: nothing in "smooth curve in ℝ^(1,3)" supplies discreteness.

(b) Superposition/interference. A single worldline is one realized history. Double-slit interference requires either a sum over many histories with complex phases (Feynman path integral — a real extension of the diagram, not a modification of it) or a wavefunction over configuration space. A lone worldline, taken as literally the whole story, cannot produce an interference pattern; there's nothing for it to interfere with.

(c) Entanglement. This is the sharpest boundary, and it's theorem-backed, not just difficult: Bell's theorem (1964, confirmed experimentally, most decisively by Aspect and collaborators — 2022 Nobel) shows no theory built from independent worldlines carrying only local information can reproduce the observed correlations between entangled particles while also respecting locality. So two independent worldline diagrams, however elaborated locally, structurally cannot reproduce entanglement. Something nonlocal has to enter, or the "independent worldlines" premise itself has to go.

4. Picking one system and finding the minimal necessary elaboration: spin
Spin is the right target because it's the one internal quantum number that's plausibly geometric rather than purely discrete — it has units of angular momentum, which the diagram already knows how to represent (as a derived quantity, from position and momentum: L = X × p). The question is whether spin can be gotten as orbital angular momentum of some structure already implicit in the curve, rather than pasted on as a new label.

Constraint: no new fundamental constants. The only quantities available are ħ, c, and the particle's own mass m. Dimensional analysis alone gives you, essentially uniquely, a length scale ħ/(mc) (Compton wavelength) and a frequency scale mc²/ħ. If the "extra structure" needed for spin must be built from the particle's own worldline using only constants already in play, it is forced, not chosen, to live at these scales. So the minimal elaboration is: superpose a small circular displacement on the center-of-mass worldline,

X^μ(τ) = X_cm^μ(τ) + r^μ(τ), r periodic, radius ~ ħ/(mc), frequency ~ mc²/ħ.
