This academic excerpt outlines a theoretical framework, the SAT model, for deriving the mass hierarchy of elementary particles based on the properties of fundamental filaments. The total effective mass ($m_{\rm eff}$) of a particle is posited as the sum of a topological component, $m_{\rm topo}$, and a smaller induced inertial component, $m_{\rm ind}$. The dominant topological mass is determined by the bare filament mass scale ($m_0$) divided by a topological invariant ($Q$), which quantifies the complexity (linking, writhe, and winding) of the filament bundle, resulting in the principle that greater topological complexity leads to smaller inertial mass. Furthermore, the model connects the particle mass spectrum to a quadratic formula involving $Q$ and a vibrational quantum number ($N$), and integrates the scalar field mass with the vacuum energy density (cosmological constant) via the same underlying filament tension scale.

\section*{Mass–Hierarchy Derivation in the SAT Framework}
\subsection*{1. Bare filament mass scale \(m_0\)}
In the SAT model the fundamental filament tension is denoted by \(T\) (units: energy per length, or \(ML\,T^{-2}\)), and the characteristic filament scale by \(\ell_f\) (length). A corresponding bare mass scale is
\[
m_0 \;\sim\; \frac{T\,\ell_f}{c^2}\,,
\]
since \(T\,\ell_f\) has units of energy (\(ML^2T^{-2}\)), dividing by \(c^2\) yields a mass (\(M\)). Thus
\[
\boxed{ \;[m_0] = M,\quad m_0= \frac{T\,\ell_f}{c^2}\,.}
\]
\subsection*{2. Topological invariant \(Q\)}
Define the topological invariant
\[
Q = \bigl(1 + |L_{\rm link}|\bigr)\;\times\;\bigl(1 + |W_{\rm writhe}|\bigr)\;\times\;(1 + n_{\rm wind})\;\times\;(1 + \deg(P_{\rm Jones}))\,,
\]
a dimensionless integer (or near-integer) classifying filament bundle topology. For simplicity one may approximate
\[
Q \approx L_{\rm link} + W_{\rm writhe} + n_{\rm wind}\,,
\]
for minimal excitations.

The dominant (“topological”) contribution to the effective mass is postulated as
\[
m_{\rm topo} = \frac{m_0}{Q}\,.
\]
This expresses the idea that the bare filament mass is suppressed by the inverse of topological complexity.

\Large\textbf{Euler--Lagrange derivations\\
for the corrected SAT / Blockwave Lagrangian}
\end{center}
\vspace{1em}
We start from the corrected, dimensionally consistent Lagrangian density (Eq.~\eqref{L} below) used in the main text:
\begin{equation}\label{L}
\mathcal{L}(\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi)
= \tfrac{1}{2}\rho \mathbf v^2
- \rho\,W(\Phi)
- \tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v),
\end{equation}
where \(W(\Phi)\) is the potential per unit mass (units of \(\Phi\)), \(\kappa\) is the curvature coupling, and \(\lambda\) is dimensionless. The independent fields for the variational principle are taken to be
\(\rho(\mathbf x,t),\,\mathbf v(\mathbf x,t),\,\Phi(\mathbf x,t)\).
Mass conservation (the continuity equation) is imposed as a constraint using a Lagrange multiplier \(\chi(\mathbf x,t)\). The constrained action is
\begin{equation}\label{S}
S[\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi,\chi]
= \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\!\!\int_{\Omega}
\Big\{ \mathcal L(\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi)
+ \chi\big(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\big)
\Big\}\,d^3x\,dt .
\end{equation}
Below we compute the stationary conditions \(\delta S=0\) under arbitrary compactly-supported variations
\(\delta\rho,\;\delta\mathbf v,\;\delta\Phi,\;\delta\chi\).
We integrate by parts and drop boundary terms (assuming either vanishing variations on the boundary or appropriate boundary conditions).
\medskip
\textbf{1. Variation with respect to the Lagrange multiplier \(\chi\).}
Varying \(S\) with respect to \(\chi\) enforces the continuity constraint:
\[
\delta_{\chi}S = \int \delta\chi\;(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v))\,d^3x\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{ \; \partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v) = 0 \; . \;}
\]
\medskip
\textbf{2. Variation with respect to the velocity field \(\mathbf v\).}
Collect \(\mathbf v\)-dependent pieces of the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset \tfrac{1}{2}\rho \mathbf v^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)
+ \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v).
\]
Take the variation \(\mathbf v \mapsto \mathbf v + \delta\mathbf v\). Compute term-by-term.
(i) Kinetic term:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(\tfrac12\rho \mathbf v^2\bigr)
= \rho\,\mathbf v\cdot\delta\mathbf v \qquad (\rho \text{ treated as independent}).
\]
(ii) Interaction term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr)
= \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\delta\mathbf v)
= -\lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi)\cdot\delta\mathbf v
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)} .
\]
(We integrated by parts once and discarded the boundary term.)
(iii) Constraint term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\bigr)
= \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\,\delta\mathbf v)
= -\rho\,\nabla\chi\cdot\delta\mathbf v
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)}.
\]
Summing (i)--(iii) and requiring stationarity for arbitrary \(\delta\mathbf v\) gives the Euler--Lagrange condition:
\[
\boxed{ \;\rho\,\mathbf v - \lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi) - \rho\,\nabla\chi \;=\; \mathbf 0 \;. \;}
\]
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{vel_eq}
\boxed{ \;\mathbf v = \nabla\chi + \frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi)\;. \;}
\end{equation}
This equation expresses the velocity field in terms of the multiplier \(\chi\) (a Bernoulli-type potential) and the interaction-gradient term. It is the canonical momentum relation coming from the variational principle.
\medskip
\textbf{3. Variation with respect to the density \(\rho\).}
Collect \(\rho\)-dependent terms from the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset \tfrac12\rho\mathbf v^2 - \rho W(\Phi) + \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v
+ \chi(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)).
\]
Vary \(\rho\mapsto\rho+\delta\rho\). Compute contributions (we treat \(\mathbf v,\Phi,\chi\) fixed while varying \(\rho\)).
(i) From kinetic term:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(\tfrac12\rho\mathbf v^2\bigr) = \tfrac12 \mathbf v^2 \,\delta\rho.
\]
(ii) From potential term:
\[
\delta(-\rho W(\Phi)) = -W(\Phi)\,\delta\rho.
\]
(iii) From interaction:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\rho\Phi\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr) = \lambda\Phi(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)\,\delta\rho.
\]
(iv) From constraint term (two pieces):
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\partial_t\rho\bigr) = \chi\,\partial_t(\delta\rho)
= -(\partial_t\chi)\,\delta\rho + \text{(boundary in time)},
\]
and
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\bigr)
= \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\delta\rho\,\mathbf v)
= -\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\chi\,\delta\rho + \text{(spatial boundary)}.
\]
Combining all contributions and discarding total time/spatial boundary terms, stationarity with respect to \(\delta\rho\) yields
\[
\tfrac12 \mathbf v^2 - W(\Phi) + \lambda\Phi(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v) - \partial_t\chi - \mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\chi = 0 .
\]
Rearrange to the more suggestive form
\begin{equation}\label{rho_eq}
\boxed{ \;\partial_t\chi + \mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\chi
= \tfrac12 \mathbf v^2 - W(\Phi) + \lambda\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)\;. \;}
\end{equation}
This is the Bernoulli (or generalized Hamilton--Jacobi) relation for the multiplier \(\chi\).
\medskip
\textbf{4. Variation with respect to the scalar field \(\Phi\).}
Collect \(\Phi\)-dependent pieces of the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset -\rho\,W(\Phi) - \tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v).
\]
Take \(\Phi\mapsto\Phi+\delta\Phi\). Compute term-by-term.
(i) Potential term:
\[
\delta\bigl(-\rho W(\Phi)\bigr) = -\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi}\,\delta\Phi .
\]
(ii) Gradient energy:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2\bigr)
= -\kappa\,\nabla\Phi\!\cdot\!\nabla(\delta\Phi)
= +\kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi\,\delta\Phi
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)},
\]
after integrating by parts once and discarding boundary contributions.
(iii) Interaction term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr)
= \lambda\,\rho\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)\,\delta\Phi .
\]
Summing these and enforcing stationarity for arbitrary \(\delta\Phi\) yields the scalar Euler--Lagrange equation
\[
-\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi + \lambda\,\rho\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v) = 0.
\]
Divide by \(\rho\) (assuming \(\rho>0\)) and rearrange to the mass-normalized form used in the main text:
\begin{equation}\label{phi_EL}
\boxed{ \;\kappa\,\frac{\nabla^2\Phi}{\rho}
- \frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \lambda\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v) = 0\;. \;}
\end{equation}
Multiplying both sides by \(\rho\) gives the compact form
\[
\boxed{ \;-\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi + \lambda\,\rho\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v = 0\;. \;}
\]
\vspace{0.5em}

If one wishes to obtain a hyperbolic (wave) evolution for \(\Phi\) with \(\partial_t^2\Phi\) present, introduce a kinetic term in \(\mathcal L\) of the form
\[
\mathcal L_{\text{kin},\Phi} = \tfrac{\mu}{2}\,(\partial_t\Phi)^2
\]
with a phenomenological coefficient \(\mu\) (with SI units chosen so \(\mu(\partial_t\Phi)^2\) is an energy density). Adding that term and redoing the \(\Phi\)-variation yields
\[
\mu\,\partial_t^2\Phi + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi - \rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \lambda\,\rho\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v = 0,
\]
which, upon dividing by \(\mu\), reproduces the mass-normalized wave-type scalar PDE used earlier (with identification \(\mu\leftrightarrow1\) up to units).
\medskip
\textbf{5. From the variational relations to the Eulerian momentum equation.}
Equations \eqref{vel_eq} and \eqref{rho_eq} are a standard Clebsch-style parametrization. To extract an explicit Eulerian momentum-balance law (the convective form written in the main text), take a material time derivative $D_t \equiv \partial_t + \mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla$ of \(\nabla\chi\) and combine with \eqref{vel_eq}--\eqref{rho_eq}. The steps (sketched) are:
\begin{align}
\mathbf v &= \nabla\chi + \frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi), \qquad\text{(from \eqref{vel_eq})}\nonumber\\
D_t\mathbf v &= D_t(\nabla\chi) + D_t\!\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi)\Big).
\end{align}
Using \(\nabla(\partial_t\chi + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla\chi)\) and substituting \eqref{rho_eq} for the combination \(\partial_t\chi + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla\chi\) yields after algebra the familiar convective momentum balance:
\[
\rho\big(\partial_t\mathbf v + (\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v\big)
= -\rho\,\nabla W(\Phi)
- \kappa\,(\nabla^2\Phi)\,\nabla\Phi
- \nabla P
+ \lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi),
\]
where the pressure term \(P\) can arise from including a thermodynamic internal-energy contribution in \(\mathcal L\) (or as a Lagrange multiplier enforcing incompressibility). The curvature body force appears precisely in the form derived earlier:
\[
\mathbf f_\kappa = -\kappa\,(\nabla^2\Phi)\,\nabla\Phi .
\]
This is the momentum equation quoted in the corrected document.
\medskip
\textbf{6. Energy identity (sketch).}
Define the total energy functional (for $\gamma=0$ and with no explicit $\partial_t\Phi$ kinetic term)
\[
E_{\text{tot}}[t] \;=\; \int_\Omega \Big( \tfrac12\rho\mathbf v^2 + \rho W(\Phi) + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2 \Big)\,d^3x.
\]
Differentiate \(E_{\text{tot}}\) with respect to time and use the continuity equation, momentum balance and the scalar EL relation to rearrange terms. After integrations by parts and assuming vanishing boundary fluxes one obtains \(dE_{\text{tot}}/dt = 0\). (If a damping term or explicit $\Phi$-kinetic dissipation is present, the right-hand side is negative definite and energy decays accordingly.)

\textbf{Summary of final EL system (compact):}
\begin{align*}
&\text{Continuity:}\qquad \partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v) = 0,\\[4pt]
&\text{Momentum (convective form):}\qquad
\rho\big(\partial_t\mathbf v + (\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla)\mathbf v\big)
= -\nabla P - \rho\,\nabla W(\Phi) - \kappa\,(\nabla^2\Phi)\,\nabla\Phi + \lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi),\\[4pt]
&\text{Scalar EL:}\qquad -\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi + \lambda\,\rho\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v = 0.
\end{align*}

This document provides a detailed, technical derivation of Noether's Theorem in two fundamental physics contexts: finite-dimensional Lagrangian mechanics (for particle systems) and classical field theory (the standard general form). The central idea is that if a continuous, infinitesimal transformation leaves the action of the system invariant (up to a total derivative or divergence), this invariance must lead to a conserved quantity (a conserved charge or current). The proof structure systematically calculates the variation of the action under an assumed symmetry, uses the Euler–Lagrange equations (the equations of motion) to simplify the result, and identifies the remaining expression as a divergence that must vanish on-shell, thereby defining the conserved current, $J^\mu$. For field theory, the text specifically notes that spacetime translations yield the canonical energy–momentum tensor, demonstrating the profound link between symmetry and conservation laws.

\section*{A. Finite-dimensional Lagrangian mechanics}
Let the configuration coordinates be \(q^i(t)\) with Lagrangian \(L(q,\dot q,t)\). The action is
\[
S[q] = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(q(t),\dot q(t),t)\,dt.
\]
\subsection*{Symmetry hypothesis}
Suppose there is a one-parameter family of transformations
\[
q^i(t) \mapsto q^i(t) + \varepsilon\,\Delta q^i(q,t) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),
\qquad t \mapsto t + \varepsilon\,\Delta t(q,t) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),
\]

\[
L(q+\varepsilon\Delta q,\dot q + \varepsilon \Delta\dot q, t+\varepsilon\Delta t)
= L(q,\dot q,t) + \varepsilon\,\frac{d}{dt}F(q,t) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2),
\]
for some function \(F(q,t)\). (When \(F=0\) the Lagrangian is strictly invariant.)
\subsection*{Variation of the action}
Compute the first-order change in \(S\) under the infinitesimal transformation. Let \(\delta q^i = \varepsilon\Delta q^i\) and \(\delta t = \varepsilon\Delta t\). The induced variation of the action (keeping fixed integration endpoints in the original parameter \(t\)) is
\[
\delta S = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \Big( \frac{\partial L}{\partial q^i}\delta q^i
+ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta\dot q^i + \frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\delta t \Big)\,dt.
\]
Integrate the $\delta\dot q^i$ term by parts:
\[
\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta\dot q^i\,dt
= \left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta q^i\right|_{t_1}^{t_2}
- \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{d}{dt}\!\Big(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Big)\delta q^i\,dt.
\]
Collecting terms and using the Euler–Lagrange equations
\(\displaystyle \frac{d}{dt}\!\Big(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Big) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q^i} = 0\),
the bulk integral vanishes for variations that follow the equations of motion. The remaining boundary contribution plus explicit total-derivative change of the Lagrangian (the \(F\)-term) yields
\[
\delta S = \left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\delta q^i\right|_{t_1}^{t_2}
+ \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(F - L\,\delta t\bigr)\,dt.
\]
Thus, for an on-shell trajectory (satisfying EOM) and when the action is invariant (i.e. $\delta S=0$ for arbitrary fixed-endpoint symmetry variations), we get
\[
\left.\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Delta q^i - F + L\,\Delta t\right)\right|_{t_1}^{t_2} = 0.
\]
Because the times \(t_1,t_2\) are arbitrary, the quantity in parentheses is constant in time. Define the Noether charge (conserved quantity)
\[
Q \;=\; \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q^i}\Delta q^i - F + L\,\Delta t.
\]
Then \(dQ/dt = 0\) along solutions of the Euler--Lagrange equations. This is Noether's theorem in mechanics.

where \(\hat\Delta\phi^a(x) = \Delta\phi^a(x) - \Xi^\mu(x)\partial_\mu\phi^a(x)\) (this subtracts the effect of the coordinate change).

\item Use the Euler--Lagrange equations to kill bulk terms; the remaining piece is a spacetime divergence.
\item Define the Noether current as the combination appearing inside the divergence; invariance implies its divergence vanishes on-shell.
\item Integrate $J^0$ over spatial slice to obtain a conserved charge.
\end{enumerate}

\Large\textbf{Euler--Lagrange derivations\\
for the corrected SAT / Blockwave Lagrangian}
\end{center}
\vspace{1em}
We start from the corrected, dimensionally consistent Lagrangian density (Eq.~\eqref{L} below) used in the main text:
\begin{equation}\label{L}
\mathcal{L}(\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi)
= \tfrac{1}{2}\rho \mathbf v^2
- \rho\,W(\Phi)
- \tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v),
\end{equation}
where \(W(\Phi)\) is the potential per unit mass (units of \(\Phi\)), \(\kappa\) is the curvature coupling, and \(\lambda\) is dimensionless. The independent fields for the variational principle are taken to be
\(\rho(\mathbf x,t),\,\mathbf v(\mathbf x,t),\,\Phi(\mathbf x,t)\).
Mass conservation (the continuity equation) is imposed as a constraint using a Lagrange multiplier \(\chi(\mathbf x,t)\). The constrained action is
\begin{equation}\label{S}
S[\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi,\chi]
= \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\!\!\int_{\Omega}
\Big\{ \mathcal L(\rho,\mathbf v,\Phi)
+ \chi\big(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\big)
\Big\}\,d^3x\,dt .
\end{equation}
Below we compute the stationary conditions \(\delta S=0\) under arbitrary compactly-supported variations
\(\delta\rho,\;\delta\mathbf v,\;\delta\Phi,\;\delta\chi\).
We integrate by parts and drop boundary terms (assuming either vanishing variations on the boundary or appropriate boundary conditions).
\medskip
\textbf{1. Variation with respect to the Lagrange multiplier \(\chi\).}
Varying \(S\) with respect to \(\chi\) enforces the continuity constraint:
\[
\delta_{\chi}S = \int \delta\chi\;(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v))\,d^3x\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{ \; \partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v) = 0 \; . \;}
\]
\medskip
\textbf{2. Variation with respect to the velocity field \(\mathbf v\).}
Collect \(\mathbf v\)-dependent pieces of the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset \tfrac{1}{2}\rho \mathbf v^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)
+ \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v).
\]
Take the variation \(\mathbf v \mapsto \mathbf v + \delta\mathbf v\). Compute term-by-term.
(i) Kinetic term:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(\tfrac12\rho \mathbf v^2\bigr)
= \rho\,\mathbf v\cdot\delta\mathbf v \qquad (\rho \text{ treated as independent}).
\]
(ii) Interaction term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr)
= \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\delta\mathbf v)
= -\lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi)\cdot\delta\mathbf v
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)} .
\]
(We integrated by parts once and discarded the boundary term.)
(iii) Constraint term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\bigr)
= \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\,\delta\mathbf v)
= -\rho\,\nabla\chi\cdot\delta\mathbf v
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)}.
\]
Summing (i)--(iii) and requiring stationarity for arbitrary \(\delta\mathbf v\) gives the Euler--Lagrange condition:
\[
\boxed{ \;\rho\,\mathbf v - \lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi) - \rho\,\nabla\chi \;=\; \mathbf 0 \;. \;}
\]
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{vel_eq}
\boxed{ \;\mathbf v = \nabla\chi + \frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi)\;. \;}
\end{equation}
This equation expresses the velocity field in terms of the multiplier \(\chi\) (a Bernoulli-type potential) and the interaction-gradient term. It is the canonical momentum relation coming from the variational principle.
\medskip
\textbf{3. Variation with respect to the density \(\rho\).}
Collect \(\rho\)-dependent terms from the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset \tfrac12\rho\mathbf v^2 - \rho W(\Phi) + \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v
+ \chi(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)).
\]
Vary \(\rho\mapsto\rho+\delta\rho\). Compute contributions (we treat \(\mathbf v,\Phi,\chi\) fixed while varying \(\rho\)).
(i) From kinetic term:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(\tfrac12\rho\mathbf v^2\bigr) = \tfrac12 \mathbf v^2 \,\delta\rho.
\]
(ii) From potential term:
\[
\delta(-\rho W(\Phi)) = -W(\Phi)\,\delta\rho.
\]
(iii) From interaction:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\rho\Phi\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr) = \lambda\Phi(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)\,\delta\rho.
\]
(iv) From constraint term (two pieces):
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\partial_t\rho\bigr) = \chi\,\partial_t(\delta\rho)
= -(\partial_t\chi)\,\delta\rho + \text{(boundary in time)},
\]
and
\[
\delta\bigl(\chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\bigr)
= \chi\,\nabla\!\cdot(\delta\rho\,\mathbf v)
= -\mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\chi\,\delta\rho + \text{(spatial boundary)}.
\]
Combining all contributions and discarding total time/spatial boundary terms, stationarity with respect to \(\delta\rho\) yields
\[
\tfrac12 \mathbf v^2 - W(\Phi) + \lambda\Phi(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v) - \partial_t\chi - \mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\chi = 0 .
\]
Rearrange to the more suggestive form
\begin{equation}\label{rho_eq}
\boxed{ \;\partial_t\chi + \mathbf v\!\cdot\!\nabla\chi
= \tfrac12 \mathbf v^2 - W(\Phi) + \lambda\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)\;. \;}
\end{equation}
This is the Bernoulli (or generalized Hamilton--Jacobi) relation for the multiplier \(\chi\).
\medskip
\textbf{4. Variation with respect to the scalar field \(\Phi\).}
Collect \(\Phi\)-dependent pieces of the integrand:
\[
\mathcal L \supset -\rho\,W(\Phi) - \tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2
+ \lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v).
\]
Take \(\Phi\mapsto\Phi+\delta\Phi\). Compute term-by-term.
(i) Potential term:
\[
\delta\bigl(-\rho W(\Phi)\bigr) = -\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi}\,\delta\Phi .
\]
(ii) Gradient energy:
\[
\delta\!\bigl(-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\nabla\Phi)^2\bigr)
= -\kappa\,\nabla\Phi\!\cdot\!\nabla(\delta\Phi)
= +\kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi\,\delta\Phi
\;+\;\text{(boundary term)},
\]
after integrating by parts once and discarding boundary contributions.
(iii) Interaction term:
\[
\delta\bigl(\lambda\,\rho\,\Phi\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v\bigr)
= \lambda\,\rho\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v)\,\delta\Phi .
\]
Summing these and enforcing stationarity for arbitrary \(\delta\Phi\) yields the scalar Euler--Lagrange equation
\[
-\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi + \lambda\,\rho\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v) = 0.
\]
Divide by \(\rho\) (assuming \(\rho>0\)) and rearrange to the mass-normalized form used in the main text:
\begin{equation}\label{phi_EL}
\boxed{ \;\kappa\,\frac{\nabla^2\Phi}{\rho}
- \frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \lambda\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v) = 0\;. \;}
\end{equation}
Multiplying both sides by \(\rho\) gives the compact form
\[
\boxed{ \;-\rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi + \lambda\,\rho\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v = 0\;. \;}
\]

\]
with a phenomenological coefficient \(\mu\) (with SI units chosen so \(\mu(\partial_t\Phi)^2\) is an energy density). Adding that term and redoing the \(\Phi\)-variation yields
\[
\mu\,\partial_t^2\Phi + \kappa\,\nabla^2\Phi - \rho\,\frac{dW}{d\Phi} + \lambda\,\rho\,\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf v = 0,
\]
which, upon dividing by \(\mu\), reproduces the mass-normalized wave-type scalar PDE used earlier (with identification \(\mu\leftrightarrow1\) up to units).

\textbf{5. From the variational relations to the Eulerian momentum equation.}

The steps (sketched) are:
\begin{align}
\mathbf v &= \nabla\chi + \frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi), \qquad\text{(from \eqref{vel_eq})}\nonumber\\
D_t\mathbf v &= D_t(\nabla\chi) + D_t\!\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\nabla(\rho\Phi)\Big).
\end{align}
Using \(\nabla(\partial_t\chi + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla\chi)\) and substituting \eqref{rho_eq} for the combination \(\partial_t\chi + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla\chi\) yields after algebra the familiar convective momentum balance:
\[
\rho\big(\partial_t\mathbf v + (\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v\big)
= -\rho\,\nabla W(\Phi)
- \kappa\,(\nabla^2\Phi)\,\nabla\Phi
- \nabla P
+ \lambda\,\nabla(\rho\Phi),
\]
where the pressure term \(P\) can arise from including a thermodynamic internal-energy contribution in \(\mathcal L\) (or as a Lagrange multiplier enforcing incompressibility). The curvature body force appears precisely in the form derived earlier:
\[
\mathbf f_\kappa = -\kappa\,(\nabla^2\Phi)\,\nabla\Phi .
\]
This is the momentum equation quoted in the corrected document.

\textbf{6. Energy identity (sketch).}
Define the total energy functional (for $\gamma=0$ and with no explicit $\partial_t\Phi$ kinetic term)
\[

Source guide
This document provides a detailed, technical derivation of Noether's Theorem in two fundamental physics contexts: finite-dimensional Lagrangian mechanics (for particle systems) and classical field theory (the standard general form). The central idea is that if a continuous, infinitesimal transformation leaves the action of the system invariant (up to a total derivative or divergence), this invariance must lead to a conserved quantity (a conserved charge or current). The proof structure systematically calculates the variation of the action under an assumed symmetry, uses the Euler–Lagrange equations (the equations of motion) to simplify the result, and identifies the remaining expression as a divergence that must vanish on-shell, thereby defining the conserved current, $J^\mu$. For field theory, the text specifically notes that spacetime translations yield the canonical energy–momentum tensor, demonstrating the profound link between symmetry and conservation laws.
