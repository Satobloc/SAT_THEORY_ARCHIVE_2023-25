
SAT Ω (Stratified Angular Tension – Omega)
Full Theory Overview
Date: 2025-08-31

------------------------------------------------------------
I. ONTOLOGICAL FOUNDATION
------------------------------------------------------------
SAT Ω posits that the fundamental constituents of the universe are 1D tensioned filaments vibrating in 4D spacetime. Unlike previous SAT models, SAT Ω holds that the filament’s interaction with the propagating time surface (Σₜ) is not merely geometric but dynamical.

The key shift: the filament's vibration draws a helical trace across Σₜ. That trace is not just a record—it becomes a real, physical structure, which can persist, lock, and participate in observable dynamics. The trace is where particle identity, stability, mass, and decay emerge.

------------------------------------------------------------
II. CORE CONCEPTS
------------------------------------------------------------
1. FILAMENT
- A tensioned, vibrating 1D object in 4D spacetime.
- Has quantized torsion (τ = nħ), curvature (κ), and incidence angle (θ₄) with Σₜ.
- Carries intrinsic tension T_f.

2. TIME SURFACE Σₜ
- A 3D surface propagating through spacetime.
- Slices filaments and accumulates their intersecting trace.
- Physically real, and resists deformation—cutting through it costs energy.

3. TRACE
- The helical structure deposited on Σₜ by the filament’s vibration.
- Physically real and persistent.
- Becomes the site of locking, mass, decay, and identity.
- May exert feedback on the filament, introducing coupled dynamics.

4. LOCKING
- When a trace achieves a geometrically resonant form (in curvature, torsion, and θ₄), it becomes persistent.
- This condition defines particle identity and stability.

------------------------------------------------------------
III. DYNAMICS AND ENERGY TRANSFER
------------------------------------------------------------
- Cutting through the time surface requires energy: trace formation is dissipative.
- Trace stores energy in its helical geometry.
- Feedback from trace may alter the filament’s vibration—introducing nonlocal effects.
- Decay occurs when the trace becomes geometrically unstable or tension is no longer sustained.

------------------------------------------------------------
IV. MASS AND IDENTITY
------------------------------------------------------------
- Mass arises from the angle of intersection: m = A · sin²(θ₄), with A ~ 5×10⁶ MeV.
- Spin derives from quantized torsion τ.
- Composite structures (e.g., hadrons) are coherent trace bundles with phase-locked helices.

------------------------------------------------------------
V. INTERACTIONS AND STRUCTURE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Binding occurs through phase locking of traces.
- Interactions between particles are mediated by geometry of overlapping trace fields.
- Electromagnetism, weak/strong forces arise from resonance and coupling patterns among traces.

------------------------------------------------------------

SAT Ω marks a full ontological and dynamical shift—now prioritizing the trace as the primary physical object and the time surface as a real, resistive frontier.
