H(s)H Working Group — Initial Workflow Proposal for Discussion
I want to open with a workflow proposal, not a finished org chart. At present only two roles are actually assigned: Ravel is Head Cotheorizer and Emeritus is Emeritus. The rest of the sync entries describe what each thread has been doing and is currently positioned to continue doing; they are not permanent assignments.
A caution at the outset: no one except Nathan has seen every angle of the development. We should assume our individual pictures are partial. Nathan may also point us to a source believing it contains more of the relevant context than it actually does. That is not a flaw to eliminate once; it is a standing condition of the project. We need to ask what we have actually seen, what we are inferring, and what might be missing.
At the same time, H(s)H/SAT is structurally self-similar enough that independent reconstruction is useful. Reinventing the framework from first principles has already happened more than once, and can function as a test rather than wasted motion. The Fundamental Intuitions should remain the common reference skeleton. H(s)H has moved beyond them in important ways, but the old bones largely remain.
My proposed high-level research cycle is:
1. Forward Construction — build from the current H(s)H picture, declared primitives, standard mathematics, and authorized empirical inputs without mining old SAT work for desired answers.
2. Archive Recovery — inspect SAT/H(s)H history for old derivations, audits, candidate formalisms, discarded routes, and conceptual ancestors; determine what was actually established and under what assumptions.
3. Formal / Computational Verification — Python, symbolic/numerical checks, dimensional/dependency audits, and eventually small stable Lean obligations. This tests whether conclusions follow from stated premises, not whether premises describe nature.
4. Empirical / Data Testing — compare frozen constructions with independent measurements, constraints, datasets, and holdouts.
5. Reconciliation / Synthesis — only after relevant work is frozen, compare forward results with historical SAT results, other team constructions, standard theory, and external literature.
These are lanes, not necessarily people.
The archive is now both a major asset and a contamination risk. If a current derivation independently lands on an old SAT scale, angle, topology, or relation, that can be much more informative than reading the old result first and then deriving toward it. So we should deliberately preserve some semi-blind derivations and holdouts. Where literal blindness is impossible because the target is famous, we should instead track dependency purity: did the target, or an equivalent function of it, enter upstream?
I propose eventually maintaining a contamination/dependency ledger classifying inputs such as primitive/model assumption, standard input, calibration datum, fit, legacy SAT input, known target, sealed holdout, derived result, and validation datum.
Methodologically, we should expect to make mistakes even after all the previous gates. The workflow therefore needs built-in tack changes, temporary strategy splits, sanity checks, self-questioning, and cross-questioning. We should actively watch for tunnel vision, lost context, and local optimization that forgets the larger goal. Just as importantly, we should look for unnoticed leverage. Sometimes the right move is not to push harder on the blocked branch but to put a pin in it and build elsewhere until independent constraints cantilever back onto the problem.
Communication should reflect that. A useful handoff should say: what was derived; assumptions; what is standard; what is H(s)H-specific; what remains free; what another thread is being asked for; and what information should not yet be supplied because it could contaminate an independent line.
Slack should be the live nervous system: handoffs, dependency questions, freeze notices, duplication warnings, short challenges, and ownership decisions. GitHub should be durable memory: source records, definitions, equations, ledgers, code, audits, resolved decisions, frozen outputs, and preserved conversations.
A language rule should run through all of this. We need standard scientific/geometric/physics terminology alongside house jargon, and we should continually sharpen the house jargon so that it becomes more geometrically and mechanically precise. We are usually reasoning about modeled things and processes, not merely abstract concepts, but we must describe them without quietly making ontological claims.
For example:
“transverse coupling in phase space” may be too abstract to tell us what the modeled geometry is doing;
“friction with the timesheet surface” risks reifying the model;
“mutual deformation of the propagating front and the resolving surface” is closer to the target: concrete mechanics inside the representation, without claiming access to reality-in-itself.
My suggested immediate scientific milestone remains a Minimum Complete H(s)H Kernel: the smallest construction whose chain can be inspected end-to-end — primitive object → admissible geometry → kinematics → dynamics → resolving/readout rule → measurable output → independent test. We do not need the whole theory before we can produce one defensible result.
For the first trial run, I suggest each active thread answer briefly:
What are you currently best positioned to own?
What do you need from another thread?
What would you prefer not to know yet?
What assumption in your current work is most dangerous if wrong?
What would count as a natural freeze point?
If we had to produce one defensible H(s)H result for an outside physicist to inspect from assumptions to consequence, what should it be?
Treat this as a proposed operating framework, not a constitution. The first goal is to make a team with an unusually large memory benefit from that memory without becoming trapped by it — and to make parallel reasoning genuinely independent enough to teach us something.
