Oreo Speedwagon: Canonical Rules, Recommendations, and Leniency Measures

Core Rule

An entry combines:

1. a recognizable musician, band, composer, singer, musical act, or historically credited performer; and

2. a recognizable food, drink, ingredient, dish, brand, preparation, or culinary object.

The result must preserve enough of both sources to be recoverable.

The Primary Standard

The strongest entries make the fewest changes necessary.

Minimal edit distance is preferred, but not by itself sufficient. The result must also land quickly enough to be legible.

The ideal entry feels discovered rather than manufactured.

Examples:

• Oreo Speedwagon

• Art Garfunkugel

• Gefilte Phish

• Spam Cooke

• Fiona Applebee’s

• Qui$he

• Stevie Ray Naan

• Halloumi & Oates

• Metallicanoli

• Poutina Turner

Dual Legibility

A strong entry allows the reader to recover both components.

This does not require equal preservation.

One component may remain almost intact while the other survives only through a highly diagnostic trace.

Example:

• Qui$he

“Quiche” remains almost fully legible. Ke$ha is represented chiefly by the dollar sign. The construction works because the food word is harder to alter without damage, while the artist can be recovered from one unusually distinctive marker.

The governing principle is therefore:

Preserve the more fragile component; reduce the other to the smallest cue that still retrieves it.

Spoken and Visual Legibility

Entries may work by sound, spelling, typography, spacing, punctuation, diacritics, or some combination.

Valid mechanisms include:

• phonetic overlap

• visual overlap

• respacing

• hyphenation

• substitution of one letter or symbol

• deliberate mispronunciation

• altered stress

• strategic capitalization

• foreign-language or historical vocabulary

• brand-name recognition

• delayed realization

Examples:

• Sorbet Midler

• Sherburt Bacharach

• Ale-icia Keys

• Kurt Cobáihn Mì

• Glirariana Grande

• Rape Against the Machine, using the vegetable pronunciation

A pronunciation that is “wrong” in ordinary speech may be right for the game if the wrongness is what makes both sources simultaneously visible.

The Legibility Threshold

An entry does not need to land instantly.

There are three acceptable speeds:

Immediate

Both components are obvious on sight.

• Spam Cooke

• Stevie Ray Naan

• Black Sandwich

Delayed

The reader pauses, then the structure clicks.

• Tom Petty Four

• Gazey Star

• Parfait Wong

• Paula Abdulce de Leche

Revealed

The entry requires explanation, but becomes satisfying once explained.

• Glirariana Grande

• Jujubal

• Enhead-cheeseuana

• Oливье Ньютон-Джон

A revealed entry is valid when the explanation exposes a real mechanism rather than retroactively inventing one.

Canonical Recommendations

Prefer:

• one clean splice over several appended words

• existing food terms over invented food-like syllables

• exact preservation of unusual names, spellings, or symbols

• a single decisive mutation

• constructions that retain the rhythm of the original name

• entries that remain speakable

• entries whose oddness contributes to the joke

• obscure but real foods when the musician remains clear

• obscure musicians when the food remains clear

• elegant asymmetry over forced symmetry

Avoid, when possible:

• adding a random food word to the end

• replacing so much of the name that the source disappears

• inventing a food solely to rescue the construction

• explaining away a broken splice with enthusiasm

• relying on a connection that exists only in the constructor’s head

• overengineering a name past the point of audible recovery

• confusing a song title with a performer unless the category has explicitly broadened

Frank Sinatra Hamburgers

A Frank Sinatra Hamburger is a performer or band name with an arbitrary food item attached, without meaningful phonetic, visual, structural, or semantic integration.

Examples:

• Frank Sinatra Hamburger

• Metallica Waffles

• Cannibal Corpse Cake
